# Audiences

The audience shapes vocabulary, illustrations and the closing response — never the translation lines, which follow `fidelity-rules.md` for everyone.

## christian

- Shared vocabulary may be used in prose, but each time a tradition term appears, show the plain word behind it at least once (e.g. "what our Bibles call *church* — ἐκκλησία, an assembly").
- Expect familiar translations: name where they diverge (`translation-comparison.md`) without mocking them.
- Contested readings: assume listeners hold one option; present all options fairly and let the text speak.
- Response: obedience to what the passage commands, trust in what it promises.

## non-christian

- **No unexplained insider terms.** Prefer the plain word; if an insider word must appear (a quote, a name people know), explain it in the same sentence.
- Assume no Bible knowledge. **Required:** before the first quote, one or two sentences that name *who* (writer or speaker), *where* (the place of the scene, by name, as the text or its context gives it) and *when*. Example: "In Jerusalem, in the first century, a teacher named Nicodemus comes to Jesus at night." Not a background lecture: two sentences at most.
- Illustrations from ordinary life, not church life.
- Response: an invitation to consider, ask, read, or trust — respectful, no pressure, no assumed agreement.

## mixed

- Write for the non-christian listener, and give the christian listener the "what's behind your translation" asides.
- Explain each insider term once, briefly, on first use.
- Response: two short invitations — one for those exploring, one for those already following.
