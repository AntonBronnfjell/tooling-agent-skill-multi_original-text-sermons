# Specializations

The specialization shapes **what the sermon does with the text** — never what the text says. Every specialization keeps the fidelity rules: fresh translation, options on contested readings, no doctrine sources. Pick one; if the user names none, ask (intake).

In every specialization, the Big Idea, point titles, conclusion, call to respond, applications and discussion questions must not resolve a contested identification, reading or practice the body left open — not by assertion, framing or a leading question (SKILL.md hard rule 16). Invite a response to what all options share, or name the open question. Wrong: "When you say 'Jesus is lord,' say it as Isaiah's tongue does" (after declining to decide). Right: "Paul sets Isaiah's 'every knee, every tongue' beside 'Jesus the anointed is lord'; how far that goes is the question the text leaves us."

## theology

Aim: state clearly what this passage contributes to a topic, and what it does not.

- Build each point from a clause of the passage; quote it.
- Separate three layers explicitly: what the text says / what it implies within the author's argument / what later systems have built on it (name only that such systems exist; do not adopt or argue them).
- Contested readings get the most space here: show all options with their grammar; say which the immediate context favors and how strongly.
- Close with "what this passage settles and what it leaves open".

## apologetics

Aim: answer real objections to the passage or its claims, from the evidence.

- **Each point answers one objection.** State the objection fairly in one or two sentences (as its holders would), then answer from the text, the witnesses (variants, DSS, LXX) and 1st-century usage.
- Concede what the evidence concedes (e.g. "this verse is TR-only"; "the text allows both readings"). An honest concession is stronger than an overreach.
- No straw men, no appeals to tradition or authority, no claims beyond the data (`fidelity-rules.md` §8).

## evangelistic

Aim: make the passage's message understandable to someone outside, and invite a response.

- One clear idea from the text; one call.
- **Clear call to respond** at the end, worded from the passage's own verbs (e.g. John.3.16 [GRC John.3.16] ὁ πιστεύων "the one who believes/trusts" → invite that; Acts.2.38 names two responses, Μετανοήσατε and βαπτισθήτω — invite both, rendered per `tradition-vocabulary.md` and with the options in `contested-readings.md`). Specific, unpressured, no manipulation, no promises the text doesn't make.
- Pair with `audiences.md` non-christian or mixed rules; the hook-book-look-took template suits it.

## teaching / discipleship

Aim: equip listeners to read the passage themselves.

- Walk through the passage in order; show one or two grammar/word observations the listener can reuse (e.g. "notice the tense", "who is the subject?").
- Include a short "how we know" aside: what the tool showed (usage counts, a variant) and how to check it.
- End with concrete practice for the week tied to the text's commands or claims.

## pastoral

Aim: bring the passage to people in pain, doubt, guilt or decision.

- Tone: gentle, direct, unhurried. Fewer technical asides; keep the original-language evidence in quote blocks and Notes.
- Apply what the text actually promises or commands — do not stretch a promise to cover more than its words (no totality transfer of comfort).
- Name the situation the text addresses in its own setting first, then the listener's.
- Close with the text's words as the last thing heard.
