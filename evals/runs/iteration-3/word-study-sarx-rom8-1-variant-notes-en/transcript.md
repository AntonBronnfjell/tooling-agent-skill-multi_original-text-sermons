## σάρξ in Romans 8:1: a short word study

### First, a finding about the verse itself

In the critical editions, σάρξ does not occur in Romans 8:1 at all. The verse in SBL (and the same words in NA, THGNT, WH) is:

> [GRC Rom.8.1] Οὐδὲν ἄρα νῦν κατάκριμα τοῖς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ
> Literal: none therefore now condemnation to-the [ones] in Anointed Jesus
> Natural: So now there is no condemnation for those who are in the Anointed Jesus.

The word comes in only with a clause that the Textus Receptus and the Byzantine text add:

> [GRC:TR Rom.8.1] μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν, ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα.
> Literal: not according-to flesh walking/[they] walk, but according-to spirit
> Natural: who do not walk by the flesh but by the spirit.

`variants Rom.8.1` tags each of these seven words (μή through πνεῦμα) [TR+Byz], class "late". The Byzantine text has the same words ([GRC:Byz Rom.8.1] μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν, ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα). The data holds printed editions, not manuscripts, so this is a difference between editions; I can't tell you which manuscripts are behind each one.

The same clause stands in Romans 8:4 in every edition (`variants Rom.8.4` returns no differences):

> [GRC Rom.8.4] τοῖς μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα
> Literal: to-the [ones] not according-to flesh walking but according-to spirit
> Natural: [in us,] those who do not walk by the flesh but by the spirit.

So for your class: if your Bible has "who walk not after the flesh" in 8:1 (KJV, WEB, YLT, RV1909), it follows the TR/Byz text, and those words match 8:4 word for word. If it ends 8:1 at "in Christ Jesus" (BSB), it follows the critical editions. Either way, the Greek phrase you are studying is securely in Paul's argument, three verses later.

### What σάρξ means: the usage evidence

The tool's numbers (`word σάρξ`, `usage σάρξ`): NT 147 in SBL, 150 in Byz, 151 in TR (152 in any edition); LXX 158. In LXX verses σάρξ co-occurs with Hebrew בָּשָׂר 130 times. That is same-verse co-occurrence, not word alignment. Josephus 11 and Philo 90, matched by surface form, so homographs may inflate those counts and unseen forms are missed.

The lexicon gloss is just "flesh", and the tool lists 15 Louw–Nida domain codes for the word. Those codes are the range across all its uses, not a list of meanings packed into one verse. Here are the senses the shown contexts display:

- **Physical flesh/body.** Josephus describes a woman going to her death "with the colour of her flesh unchanged": [EXT:Josephus Ant.15.236] τῇ χρόᾳ τῆς σαρκὸς ἀμεταβλήτῳ. Other Josephus contexts shown (Ant.6.120, Ant.12.211) use the plural for meat.
- **Human beings as mortal.** In the LXX of Genesis 6:3, God's spirit will not remain in people:

  > [LXX Gen.6.3] διὰ τὸ εἶναι αὐτοὺς σάρκας
  > Literal: because-of the to-be them fleshes
  > Natural: because they are flesh.

  The Hebrew there reads [HEB Gen.6.3] בְּשַׁגַּם הוּא בָשָׂר. Acts 2:17 likewise has [GRC Acts.2.17] ἐπὶ πᾶσαν σάρκα, "on all flesh", meaning all people.
- **Human descent.** Paul himself uses κατὰ σάρκα for lineage:

  > [GRC Rom.1.3] ἐκ σπέρματος Δαυὶδ κατὰ σάρκα
  > Literal: from seed of-David according-to flesh
  > Natural: descended from David by human descent.

- **Romans 8, in context.** Here σάρξ is set against πνεῦμα:

  > [GRC Rom.8.3] ἐν ᾧ ἠσθένει διὰ τῆς σαρκός
  > Literal: in which it-was-weak through the flesh
  > Natural: in that it was weak because of the flesh,

  > [GRC Rom.8.5] οἱ γὰρ κατὰ σάρκα ὄντες τὰ τῆς σαρκὸς φρονοῦσιν, οἱ δὲ κατὰ πνεῦμα τὰ τοῦ πνεύματος.
  > Literal: the [ones] for according-to flesh being the [things] of-the flesh mind/set-their-thinking-on, the [ones] but according-to spirit the [things] of-the spirit
  > Natural: For those who live by the flesh set their minds on what the flesh wants, but those who live by the spirit, on what the spirit wants.

  In these verses σάρξ is something through which the law "was weak" (8:3). It is a standard people can "walk" or "be" by (8:4, 8:5), and it is the opposite of πνεῦμα. The same chapter also uses it of the son's coming "in the likeness of flesh of sin" (8:3), so physical humanity is also in view nearby. From these contexts, "flesh" in 8:4–5 names human existence as a guide for living, in contrast with the spirit. It is not simply "the body", and not "meat". The data does not let us narrow it further than Paul's own contrasts do.

**Fallacy check.** Don't build anything on the English word "flesh" or on the full lexicon entry. Pick the sense the sentence needs (above), and don't make σάρξ = "body" in Rom.8.4–5 just because it means body in Josephus.

### Notes

- **Variant, Rom.8.1:** after Ἰησοῦ, TR and Byz add [GRC:TR Rom.8.1] μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν, ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα. Each word (#9–#15) is tagged [TR+Byz], class late. The clause is absent in SBL, NA, THGNT and WH (`verse --witness NA|THGNT|WH` end at Ἰησοῦ). Its wording matches [GRC Rom.8.4] μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα, which all editions print. Meaning: in 8:1 the TR/Byz clause describes "those in the Anointed Jesus"; without it, 8:1 has no qualifying clause.
- **Parsing, Rom.8.1 TR περιπατοῦσιν:** the tool's TR word row parses it V-PAI-3P ("they walk"). In Rom.8.4 the same form is parsed V-PAP-DPM (participle, dative plural, "to those walking"). The form allows both. In TR 8:1 it follows the dative τοῖς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ: a dative participle would agree with it, while an indicative would make the words a separate clause. Options: (a) participle "who walk", in line with the 8:4 parse; (b) indicative "they walk", as the TR row tags it. I show both.
- **Spelling variant, Rom.1.3:** Δαυεὶδ (Tyn+WH), Δαβὶδ (TR); SBL Δαυὶδ. No change in meaning.
- **Acts.2.17:** an edition difference elsewhere in the verse (ἐνύπνια TR+Byz / ἐνυπνίοις NA28+NA27+Tyn+SBL+WH+Treg) does not touch the words quoted.
- **Papyri:** the Moulton–Milligan excerpt the tool printed for σάρξ is the lexicographers' own commentary, not a papyrus citation, so I did not use it.
- **Comparison, Rom.8.1:** KJV "who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit", WEB "who don't walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit", YLT "who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit", RV1909 "los que no andan conforme á la carne, mas conforme al espíritu" [late-reading: TR/Byz clause; harmonization: wording identical to Rom.8.4]. BSB ends at "in Christ Jesus" (= SBL/NA/THGNT/WH). KJV/WEB/YLT capital "Spirit" [capitalization]; RV1909 lower-case "espíritu". KJV "[There is]" is words the KJV itself marks as added [added-words].
- **Comparison, Rom.8.4:** KJV "after the flesh … after the Spirit"; BSB, WEB, YLT "according to the flesh … according to the Spirit" [capitalization: "Spirit"]; RV1909 "conforme á la carne, mas conforme al espíritu".
