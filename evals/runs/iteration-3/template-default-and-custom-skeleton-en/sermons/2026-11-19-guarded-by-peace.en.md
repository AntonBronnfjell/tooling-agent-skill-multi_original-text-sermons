# Guarded by Peace — Philippians 4:4–9

## Opener

Imagine lying awake at three in the morning. Nothing is actually happening in the room; the house is quiet. But your mind is a storm: the bill, the diagnosis, the conversation you dread. You are not in danger tonight, and yet you feel as if the walls have no guard. Paul's words to a small assembly in Philippi speak directly into that night.

## The Word

Paul writes to "all the holy ones in the Anointed Jesus who are in Philippi" (Philippians 1:1), and he writes as a man in chains (1:13). Near the end of the letter he gives them this:

> [GRC Phil.4.4] Χαίρετε ἐν κυρίῳ πάντοτε· πάλιν ἐρῶ, χαίρετε.
> Literal: rejoice in [the] lord always; again I-will-say, rejoice
> Natural: Be glad in the lord always. I will say it again: be glad!

> [GRC Phil.4.5] τὸ ἐπιεικὲς ὑμῶν γνωσθήτω πᾶσιν ἀνθρώποις. ὁ κύριος ἐγγύς·
> Literal: the gentleness of-you let-it-be-known to-all people. the lord [is] near
> Natural: Let your gentleness be known to everyone. The lord is near.

> [GRC Phil.4.6] μηδὲν μεριμνᾶτε, ἀλλ’ ἐν παντὶ τῇ προσευχῇ καὶ τῇ δεήσει μετ’ εὐχαριστίας τὰ αἰτήματα ὑμῶν γνωριζέσθω πρὸς τὸν θεόν·
> Literal: nothing be-anxious-about, but in everything by-the prayer and by-the asking with thanks the requests of-you let-be-made-known to the God
> Natural: Don't be anxious about anything. Instead, in everything, by prayer and pleading, with thanks, let your requests be made known to God.

> [GRC Phil.4.7] καὶ ἡ εἰρήνη τοῦ θεοῦ ἡ ὑπερέχουσα πάντα νοῦν φρουρήσει τὰς καρδίας ὑμῶν καὶ τὰ νοήματα ὑμῶν ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ.
> Literal: and the peace of-the God, the [one] surpassing every mind/understanding, will-guard the hearts of-you and the thoughts of-you in Anointed Jesus
> Natural: And God's peace, which goes beyond anything the mind can grasp, will stand guard over your hearts and your thoughts in the Anointed, Jesus.

> [GRC Phil.4.8] Τὸ λοιπόν, ἀδελφοί, ὅσα ἐστὶν ἀληθῆ, ὅσα σεμνά, ὅσα δίκαια, ὅσα ἁγνά, ὅσα προσφιλῆ, ὅσα εὔφημα,
> Literal: the rest, brothers, whatever-things are true, whatever honorable, whatever right, whatever pure, whatever lovely, whatever well-spoken-of,
> Natural: Finally, brothers: whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is right, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is well spoken of —

> [GRC Phil.4.8] εἴ τις ἀρετὴ καὶ εἴ τις ἔπαινος, ταῦτα λογίζεσθε·
> Literal: if any excellence and if any praise, these-things reckon/count
> Natural: if there is any excellence, anything worth praise, take these things into account.

> [GRC Phil.4.9] ἃ καὶ ἐμάθετε καὶ παρελάβετε καὶ ἠκούσατε καὶ εἴδετε ἐν ἐμοί, ταῦτα πράσσετε·
> Literal: what-things both you-learned and you-received and you-heard and you-saw in me, these-things practice
> Natural: What you learned and received from me, what you heard and saw in me, put that into practice.

> [GRC Phil.4.9] καὶ ὁ θεὸς τῆς εἰρήνης ἔσται μεθ’ ὑμῶν.
> Literal: and the God of-the peace will-be with you
> Natural: And the God of peace will be with you.

For the first hearers, one word would have had a sharp edge: φρουρήσει, "will guard". In the New Testament the same verb describes a ruler guarding the city of Damascus to catch Paul (2 Corinthians 11:32), and Josephus, a Jewish historian of the same century, uses it for guarding cities, palaces and exits. It is the language of a garrison. Notice also the verb in verse 8, λογίζεσθε. It is a counting, reckoning word; in the Greek Old Testament it appears mostly in verses where the Hebrew has חָשַׁב, "to think, to reckon". Paul is not saying "daydream about nice things"; he is saying "count these, take them into your reckoning".

## The Anchor Point

When the requests go up to God with thanks, God's peace stands guard over the heart and the mind.

## Three Ropes

**Rope one.** Text: "Don't be anxious about anything; in everything let your requests be made known to God" — the text answers anxiety not with silence but with asking. Life: write down tonight the one thing keeping you awake and say it to God by name, with one thing you are thankful for.

**Rope two.** Text: God's peace "will stand guard" over hearts and thoughts — a garrison word. Life: when the 3 a.m. storm starts again, remind yourself that the walls do have a guard, and that you do not have to stand that watch alone.

**Rope three.** Text: "take these things into account … put that into practice" — what fills the mind and what the hands do belong together. Life: this week, choose one true, right or lovely thing to dwell on each morning, and one thing you have learned from someone faithful to do before evening.

## Response

The lord is near. Bring him the requests you have been carrying alone, with thanks, and let his peace take up its post. And then, with your thoughts guarded, go and practice what you have learned. "And the God of peace will be with you."
