# Every Knee, Every Tongue — Philippians 2:10–11 and Isaiah 45:21–25

## Introduction

Many of us can finish this sentence from memory: "…that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow." It's a line we sing and quote, and we often stop there. But Paul did not invent those words. "Every knee" and "every tongue" already stood in an older text, in a speech of Israel's God in Isaiah 45.

Tonight we'll learn to read Philippians 2:10–11 the way its first readers could have: with Isaiah open beside it. We'll read Isaiah in Hebrew and in the Greek translation (the Septuagint), and then put Paul's sentence next to them. The question we'll keep asking is simple: which words are shared, and which are new?

## Text

Three texts, one per point: Isaiah 45:21–22 (Point 1), Isaiah 45:23–25 (Point 2), Philippians 2:10–11 (Point 3). Here is Paul's sentence first, so we know what we're listening for. Paul is writing to the believers in Philippi:

> [GRC Phil.2.10] ἵνα ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ πᾶν γόνυ κάμψῃ ἐπουρανίων καὶ ἐπιγείων καὶ καταχθονίων
> Literal: that in/at the name of-Jesus every knee may-bow, of-heavenly-ones and of-earthly-ones and of-those-under-the-earth
> Natural: so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in the heavens, on earth and under the earth,

> [GRC Phil.2.11] καὶ πᾶσα γλῶσσα ἐξομολογήσηται ὅτι κύριος Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς εἰς δόξαν θεοῦ πατρός
> Literal: and every tongue may-acknowledge that lord/Lord [is] Jesus Anointed, to glory of-God father
> Natural: and every tongue should openly declare, "Jesus the Anointed is Lord," to the glory of God the father.

All six Greek editions in our data print the same words here (`variants` lists no differences). So the wording is not in question; only its meaning is.

## Big Idea

The words Isaiah gives to Israel's one God, "to me every knee will bow, every tongue …", are the words Paul uses for what will happen at the name of Jesus, and he ends the sentence with the glory of God the father.

Three things carry this: the shared words (πᾶν γόνυ, πᾶσα γλῶσσα), the verb for the tongue (ἐξομολογέω in Isaiah's Greek, ἐξομολογέομαι in Paul), and the one new phrase Paul adds: "that Jesus the Anointed is Lord."

## Point 1

**Isaiah 45:21–22: "There is no other."**

> [HEB Isa.45.21] הֲלוֹא אֲנִי יְהוָה וְאֵין־עוֹד אֱלֹהִים מִבַּלְעָדַי אֵל־צַדִּיק וּמוֹשִׁיעַ אַיִן זוּלָתִי
> Literal: is-it-not I YHWH? and-there-is-no other God/god besides-me; a-God/god righteous and-rescuing, there-is-none except-me
> Natural: Was it not I, YHWH? There is no other God besides me, a righteous God who rescues; there is none but me.

> [LXX Isa.45.21] ἐγὼ ὁ θεός καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἄλλος πλὴν ἐμοῦ δίκαιος καὶ σωτὴρ οὐκ ἔστιν πάρεξ ἐμοῦ
> Literal: I the God, and not is other except me; righteous and rescuer not is besides me
> Natural: I am God, and there is no other except me; there is no righteous one and rescuer besides me.

> [HEB Isa.45.22] פְּנוּ־אֵלַי וְהִוָּשְׁעוּ כָּל־אַפְסֵי־אָרֶץ כִּי אֲנִי־אֵל וְאֵין עוֹד
> Literal: turn to-me and-be-rescued all-ends-of earth, for I God/El and-there-is-no other
> Natural: Turn to me and be rescued, all the ends of the earth, for I am God, and there is no other.

> [LXX Isa.45.22] ἐπιστράφητε πρός με καὶ σωθήσεσθε οἱ ἀπ᾿ ἐσχάτου τῆς γῆς ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ θεός καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἄλλος
> Literal: turn to me and you-will-be-rescued, the-ones from end of-the earth; I am the God and not is other
> Natural: Turn to me and you will be rescued, you from the end of the earth; I am God, and there is no other.

Here's a reading skill you can reuse: **check who the speaker is, and what name the text uses for him in each language.** In the Hebrew of verse 21 the speaker names himself יְהוָה. In the Greek of the same verse he is ὁ θεός, "God". The Greek here doesn't use κύριος. One more detail: the Hebrew has an imperative, "be rescued," and the Greek turns it into a promise, σωθήσεσθε, "you will be rescued."

The point of these verses is exclusive: "no other … besides me … none but me." The invitation goes to "all the ends of the earth."

## Point 2

**Isaiah 45:23–25: the oath.**

> [HEB Isa.45.23] בִּי נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי יָצָא מִפִּי צְדָקָה דָּבָר וְלֹא יָשׁוּב כִּי־לִי תִּכְרַע כָּל־בֶּרֶךְ תִּשָּׁבַע כָּל־לָשׁוֹן
> Literal: by-me I-have-sworn; has-gone-out from-my-mouth righteousness, a-word, and-not it-will-return: that to-me will-bow every knee, will-swear every tongue
> Natural: By myself I have sworn; from my mouth has gone out in righteousness a word that will not turn back: to me every knee will bow, every tongue will swear.

> [LXX Isa.45.23] κατ᾿ ἐμαυτοῦ ὀμνύω ἦ μὴν ἐξελεύσεται ἐκ τοῦ στόματός μου δικαιοσύνη οἱ λόγοι μου οὐκ ἀποστραφήσονται ὅτι ἐμοὶ κάμψει πᾶν γόνυ καὶ ἐξομολογήσεται πᾶσα γλῶσσα τῷ θεῷ
> Literal: by myself I-swear: truly will-go-out from the mouth of-me righteousness; the words of-me not will-be-turned-back; that to-me will-bow every knee and will-acknowledge every tongue to-the God
> Natural: By myself I swear: righteousness will surely go out from my mouth; my words will not be turned back: to me every knee will bow, and every tongue will acknowledge God.

> [HEB Isa.45.24] אַךְ בַּיהוָה לִי אָמַר צְדָקוֹת וָעֹז עָדָיו יָבוֹא וְיֵבֹשׁוּ כֹּל הַנֶּחֱרִים בּוֹ
> Literal: only in-YHWH, of-me one-said, righteousnesses and-strength; to-him will-come and-will-be-ashamed all the-ones-angry with-him
> Natural: "Only in YHWH," they will say of me, "are right acts and strength." To him they will come, and all who rage against him will be put to shame.

> [LXX Isa.45.24] λέγων δικαιοσύνη καὶ δόξα πρὸς αὐτὸν ἥξουσιν καὶ αἰσχυνθήσονται πάντες οἱ ἀφορίζοντες ἑαυτούς
> Literal: saying: righteousness and glory to him will-come, and will-be-ashamed all the-ones separating themselves
> Natural: …saying, "Righteousness and glory will come to him," and all who separate themselves will be put to shame.

> [HEB Isa.45.25] בַּיהוָה יִצְדְּקוּ וְיִתְהַלְלוּ כָּל־זֶרַע יִשְׂרָאֵל
> Literal: in-YHWH will-be-in-the-right and-will-boast all-seed-of Israel
> Natural: In YHWH all the offspring of Israel will be in the right, and they will boast.

> [LXX Isa.45.25] ἀπὸ κυρίου δικαιωθήσονται καὶ ἐν τῷ θεῷ ἐνδοξασθήσονται πᾶν τὸ σπέρμα τῶν υἱῶν Ισραηλ
> Literal: from lord/Lord will-be-made/declared-right and in the God will-be-glorified all the seed of-the sons of-Israel
> Natural: From the Lord all the offspring of the sons of Israel will be put in the right, and in God they will be glorified.

Look at the tongue. In Hebrew it **swears**: תִּשָּׁבַע, from שָׁבַע "to swear" (the tool's gloss). In the Greek it **acknowledges**: ἐξομολογήσεται. How was that Greek verb used? `usage ἐξομολογέω` shows LXX contexts of praise and acknowledgment to God or to his name (Gen.29.35; 1Kgs.8.33). Josephus uses it for a speaker admitting his own folly (War.1.625). A papyrus cited in the Moulton–Milligan lexicon uses it for acknowledging a debt (the papyri data is OCR text and unverified). In the 92 LXX occurrences, `word ἐξομολογέω` shows the verb in the same verse as Hebrew יָדָה 80 times. That is co-occurrence, not alignment. So the Greek tongue "acknowledges openly". It is not the same verb as the Hebrew "swear", but both are spoken, public commitments.

And look at the divine name, verse by verse. Hebrew יְהוָה stands in verses 21, 24 and 25. The Greek has ὁ θεός in 21, no divine name in 24, and κυρίου in 25.

## Point 3

**Philippians 2:10–11: the same words, a new sentence.**

Now read Paul again. He shares with LXX Isaiah 45:23 "every knee", "will bow / may bow" (κάμψει, κάμψῃ) and "every tongue", plus the same acknowledging verb in another form. Paul's Greek uses ἐξομολογέομαι, and `word` counts it 10 times in the NT. Isaiah's Greek uses ἐξομολογέω, 92 times in the LXX. Our data files the NT and LXX forms under different lemmas.

What's different is the setting. In Isaiah, knees bow "to me", the speaker who says "there is no other." In Paul, knees bow "at the name of Jesus", and what the tongue acknowledges is that "Jesus the Anointed is Lord," leading "to the glory of God the father."

The word κύριος is worth slowing down for. In the `usage κύριος` contexts we looked at, it is a form of address (Acts.1.6 Κύριε), a title beside "God" in LXX Genesis 2, and, in Josephus, the word for a man put over Egypt as its master (Ant.2.174).

In our Isaiah passage, LXX 45:25 has κυρίου where the Hebrew has בַּיהוָה. Isaiah 45:23 itself, the line Paul shares, has neither name.

The text sets these side by side. How much of Isaiah 45 Paul means to bring along is a question for you to weigh with the evidence in front of you.

Paul also used the same Isaiah line in Romans:

> [GRC Rom.14.11] ὅτι ἐμοὶ κάμψει πᾶν γόνυ, καὶ πᾶσα γλῶσσα ἐξομολογήσεται τῷ θεῷ
> Literal: that to-me will-bow every knee, and every tongue will-acknowledge to-the God
> Natural: "To me every knee will bow, and every tongue will acknowledge God."

There, the wording follows the LXX closely, and he introduces it with "it is written."

## Conclusion

Isaiah's God swears an oath that cannot turn back: every knee to him, every tongue. Paul writes that same picture around the name of Jesus, and he ends it in the glory of God the father.

For practice this week: read Isaiah 45:21–25 and Philippians 2:5–11 side by side, once each day. Mark the shared words. Then write down, in your own words, what Paul added and what he kept. And when you say "Jesus is Lord," say it as Isaiah's tongue does: out loud, as a public commitment, to the glory of God.

"Every tongue should openly declare, 'Jesus the Anointed is Lord,' to the glory of God the father."
