# ἐκκλησία: a word study from the evidence

For a Christian Bible class (teaching). Everything below comes from this session's lookups in the local database: the Greek Old Testament (LXX), the Greek New Testament editions, Josephus and Philo (both 1st-century Jewish writers in Greek), and one noisy lexicon entry on papyri and inscriptions. Counts and contexts are what the tool returned; I say where that is a sample.

## 1. The numbers

- New Testament: 114 times in the SBL edition (NA 114, THGNT 114, WH 114, Byz 113, TR 116; 117 rows across all editions).
- LXX (Greek Old Testament): 77 times. In the LXX verses where it appears, the Hebrew word קָהָל stands in the same verse 66 times. That is same-verse co-occurrence, not a word-for-word alignment, but it shows the company the word keeps.
- Josephus: 48; Philo: 23. (The tool finds these by matching word forms, so the figures can be slightly high or low.)
- The tool's lexicon gloss is simply "assembly".

## 2. Before the New Testament: the Greek Old Testament

In the LXX the word names Israel gathered before God, above all the gathering at Horeb/Sinai:

> [LXX Deut.9.10] ἐν τῷ ὄρει ἡμέρᾳ ἐκκλησίας
> Literal: on the mountain on-day of-assembly
> Natural: on the mountain, on the day of the assembly

The Hebrew text at that spot reads [HEB Deut.9.10] בְּיוֹם הַקָּהָל, "on the day of the assembly". In Deut 4:10 the LXX even uses the related verb:

> [LXX Deut.4.10] ἐκκλησίασον πρός με τὸν λαόν
> Literal: assemble to me the people
> Natural: gather the people to me

There the Hebrew has [HEB Deut.4.10] הַקְהֶל־לִי אֶת־הָעָם, "assemble for me the people".

But the word is not reserved for Israel at worship. Here it describes a gathering the psalmist wants nothing to do with:

> [LXX Ps.26.5] ἐμίσησα ἐκκλησίαν πονηρευομένων
> Literal: I-hated assembly of-evildoers
> Natural: I hate the gathering of those who do evil

The Hebrew is [HEB Ps.26.5] שָׂנֵאתִי קְהַל מְרֵעִים. So in the LXX, what makes an ἐκκλησία good or bad is who gathers and why; the word itself just means the gathering.

## 3. Around the New Testament: Josephus and Philo

Josephus uses the word for Moses gathering the people to hear God's words:

> [EXT:Josephus Ant.3.84] συγκαλεῖ τὸ πλῆθος εἰς ἐκκλησίαν ἀκουσόμενον ὧν ὁ θεὸς εἴποι πρὸς αὐτόν
> Literal: he-calls-together the multitude into assembly, to-hear the-things-which the God might-say to him
> Natural: he calls the people together into an assembly to hear what God had said to him

He also uses it for later gatherings of the people, one where Ananus addresses the crowd (War 4.162) and one that the king calls in Jerusalem (Ant 16.62):

> [EXT:Josephus War.4.162] συνελθόντος τοῦ πλήθους εἰς ἐκκλησίαν
> Literal: having-come-together the multitude into assembly
> Natural: when the people had come together into an assembly

> [EXT:Josephus Ant.16.62] κἀκεῖθεν ἐλθὼν εἰς Ἱεροσόλυμα συνήγαγεν ἐκκλησίαν πάνδημον
> Literal: and-from-there having-come to Jerusalem he-gathered assembly of-all-the-people
> Natural: from there he came to Jerusalem and gathered an assembly of the whole people

Philo uses it for Israel at Sinai, and notes who was there:

> [EXT:Philo Decal.1.32] ἀθροισθέντος τοῦ ἔθνους ἀνδρῶν ὁμοῦ καὶ γυναικῶν εἰς ἐκκλησίαν
> Literal: having-been-gathered the nation, men together and women, into assembly
> Natural: when the nation, men and women together, had been gathered into an assembly

And he uses it for ordinary civic life in Greek cities:

> [EXT:Philo Prob.1.138] βουλαί τε γὰρ καὶ ἐκκλησίαι καθ’ ἑκάστην σχεδὸν ἡμέραν ἀθροίζονται
> Literal: councils both for and assemblies according-to each nearly day are-gathered
> Natural: for councils and assemblies meet almost every day

(Only the usage of the word is taken from Philo here, not his interpretation of the passages.)

Papyri and inscriptions: the tool's Moulton–Milligan entry is noisy OCR of a printed lexicon, so I only paraphrase the ancient evidence it cites and leave aside its own commentary. It cites a bilingual inscription from the theatre at Ephesus, dated in the entry to AD 103–4, about statues to be set up in the theatre for the assemblies, and an inscription from Apamea about an assembly of the whole people. Treat these as pointers, not verified text.

## 4. In the New Testament

The same word describes a civic crowd in Ephesus, with no religious sense at all:

> [GRC Acts.19.32] ἦν γὰρ ἡ ἐκκλησία συγκεχυμένη
> Literal: was for the assembly confused
> Natural: for the assembly was in confusion

> [GRC Acts.19.39] ἐν τῇ ἐννόμῳ ἐκκλησίᾳ ἐπιλυθήσεται
> Literal: in the lawful assembly it-will-be-settled
> Natural: it will be settled in the regular, lawful assembly

In Acts 7:38 it is used for Israel at Sinai, just as the LXX uses it:

> [GRC Acts.7.38] ἐν τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ
> Literal: in the assembly in the wilderness
> Natural: in the assembly in the wilderness

In Paul's letters it names the believers who gather, sometimes in a house:

> [GRC Rom.16.5] καὶ τὴν κατ’ οἶκον αὐτῶν ἐκκλησίαν
> Literal: and the at house of-them assembly
> Natural: and the assembly that meets at their house

> [GRC 1Cor.11.18] συνερχομένων ὑμῶν ἐν ἐκκλησίᾳ
> Literal: coming-together you in assembly
> Natural: when you come together as an assembly

> [GRC 1Cor.1.2] τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ τοῦ θεοῦ … τῇ οὔσῃ ἐν Κορίνθῳ
> Literal: to-the assembly of-the God … the [one] being in Corinth
> Natural: to God's assembly … the one in Corinth

Hebrews quotes Psalm 22:22 from the LXX, where the Hebrew has קָהָל:

> [GRC Heb.2.12] ἐν μέσῳ ἐκκλησίας ὑμνήσω σε
> Literal: in middle of-assembly I-will-sing-hymns-to you
> Natural: in the middle of the assembly I will sing your praise

In the Gospels, the word appears in this data only at Matt 16:18 and Matt 18:17 (16:18 has its own contested question about "this rock", which is outside this study).

## 5. What the evidence shows, and what it doesn't

- In the contexts shown here (LXX Deuteronomy and Psalms, Josephus, Philo, Acts, Romans, 1 Corinthians, Hebrews), ἐκκλησία names a gathering of people: Israel before God, a civic assembly, a crowd in a theatre, a group of believers meeting in a house or a city. What kind of gathering it is comes from the context ("of God", "of evildoers", "lawful", "at their house"), not from the word.
- None of these contexts shows the meaning "the called-out ones". That idea comes from splitting the word into ἐκ + καλέω, which is the root fallacy: a word means what its users meant by it, and the usage here points to "assembly, gathering". These examples are a sample; they don't tell us how every first-century hearer understood the word.
- "Church" is a later church word, not a rendering drawn from the usage shown here. The KJV itself shows the issue: for the very same Greek word it says "church in the wilderness" at Acts 7:38 but "the assembly was confused" at Acts 19:32. YLT and WEB say "assembly" in both places; RV1909 has "congregación" (Acts 7:38), "concurrencia" (19:32) and "asamblea" (19:39), but "iglesia" at Rom 16:5. When you read "church" in your Bible, the Greek underneath is this ordinary word for an assembly.

## 6. For the class

- Exercise: give the class Acts 7:38, Acts 19:32 and Rom 16:5 in two translations and ask them to find the one Greek word behind three different English renderings.
- Discussion: if ἐκκλησία is "the people gathered", what does that suggest about a group that never actually gathers? Let the texts above, especially 1 Cor 11:18, answer.
