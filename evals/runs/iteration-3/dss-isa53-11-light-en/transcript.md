Here is Isaiah 53:11 in each witness the database holds, with a literal and a natural English line under every quote. You heard right: some witnesses have "light" in this verse, and some don't. Below is what each one shows, without picking a winner.

## 1. The Hebrew (Masoretic text, WLC)

> [HEB Isa.53.11] מֵעֲמַל נַפְשׁוֹ יִרְאֶה יִשְׂבָּע בְּדַעְתּוֹ יַצְדִּיק צַדִּיק עַבְדִּי לָרַבִּים וַעֲוֹנֹתָם הוּא יִסְבֹּל
> Literal: from-the-labor-of his-soul/self he-will-see he-will-be-satisfied; by-his-knowledge will-make/declare-right [the] righteous-one my-servant for-the-many, and-their-iniquities he will-bear
> Natural: After the toil of his soul he will see [and] be satisfied; by his knowledge the righteous one, my servant, will put many in the right, and he will carry their iniquities.

Things to notice for a study group:

- **"He will see" has no object here.** יִרְאֶה ("he will see") is followed directly by יִשְׂבָּע ("he will be satisfied"). English versions have to supply something: KJV adds "[and]" in brackets; YLT puts a dash ("he seeth — he is satisfied").
- **The verbs are yiqtol** (the tool tags all of them `Vqi3ms`/`Vhi3ms`): incomplete or future action, which is why the glosses read "he will see … he will bear".
- **יַצְדִּיק** is the hiphil (causative) of צָדֵק. The tool's gloss is "to justify"; the causative form means "make right" or "declare right / put in the right". The verse itself doesn't say which; I've kept "put … in the right", which covers both.
- **צַדִּיק עַבְדִּי** can be read "my righteous servant" or "the righteous one, my servant".

## 2. The Dead Sea Scrolls

The `dss` tool returns **four** scrolls for this verse. Here is each one. The scrolls hold extant letters only; letters the editors reconstructed are not quoted as scroll text.

**1QIsaa** has the whole verse:

> [DSS:1QIsaa Isa.53.11] מעמל נפשוה יראה אור וישבע ובדעתו
> Literal: from-the-labor-of his-soul he-will-see light and-he-will-be-satisfied and-by-his-knowledge
> Natural: After the toil of his soul he will see light and be satisfied, and by his knowledge…

> [DSS:1QIsaa Isa.53.11] יצדיק צדיק עבדו לרבים
> Literal: will-make/declare-right [the] righteous-one his-servant for-the-many
> Natural: …the righteous one, his servant, will put many in the right.

So 1QIsaa differs from the Masoretic text twice in this verse: it has אור "light" after "he will see", and עבדו "his servant" where the Masoretic text has עַבְדִּי "my servant".

**1Q8** also reads "light":

> [DSS:1Q8 Isa.53.11] מעמל נפשו יראה אור
> Literal: from-the-labor-of his-soul he-will-see light
> Natural: After the toil of his soul he will see light.

The middle of the verse is lost in 1Q8 (the tool marks the gap with `…`), but where the text resumes it reads [DSS:1Q8 Isa.53.11] עבדי לרם, "my servant" like the Masoretic text.

**4Q58** is damaged at exactly this spot:

> [DSS:4Q58 Isa.53.11] מעמל נפשו יראה או ושבע
> Literal: from-the-labor-of his-soul he-will-see li… and-be-satisfied
> Natural: After the toil of his soul he will see li[ght] and be satisfied.

Only the letters או survive. The tool's word list shows the editors' reconstruction או[ר]: the last letter is supplied by the editors, so only the first two letters of the word are on the scroll.

Later in the verse 4Q58 has עבדי "my servant", and after לר there is a gap where words are lost.

**4Q56** preserves only the end of the verse: [DSS:4Q56 Isa.53.11] א יסב, a letter and part of "he will bear". It has nothing at the place where "light" would stand, so it is no witness either way.

## 3. The Septuagint (Greek, Rahlfs)

In the Greek, verse 11 continues a sentence that starts at the end of verse 10:

> [LXX Isa.53.10] καὶ βούλεται κύριος ἀφελεῖν
> Literal: and wills [the]-Lord to-take-away
> Natural: and the Lord wills to take away…

(In the Hebrew of 53:10 the name יְהוָה stands in this part of the verse; the Greek has κύριος.)

> [LXX Isa.53.11] ἀπὸ τοῦ πόνου τῆς ψυχῆς αὐτοῦ δεῖξαι αὐτῷ φῶς καὶ πλάσαι τῇ συνέσει
> Literal: from the toil of-the soul of-him, to-show to-him light and to-form with-the understanding
> Natural: …from the toil of his soul, to show him light and to shape [him] with understanding,

> [LXX Isa.53.11] δικαιῶσαι δίκαιον εὖ δουλεύοντα πολλοῖς καὶ τὰς ἁμαρτίας αὐτῶν αὐτὸς ἀνοίσει
> Literal: to-make/declare-right a-righteous-one well serving many; and the sins of-them he will-carry
> Natural: to put in the right a righteous one who serves many well; and he himself will carry their sins.

The Greek has φῶς "light" after the "seeing" idea, as 1QIsaa and 1Q8 do, but the whole verse is built differently: the Lord is the one who acts ("to show him light"), with a chain of infinitives ("to show … to shape … to put in the right") that depend on βούλεται "wills" in v.10.

"My servant" doesn't appear as a noun; in its place is εὖ δουλεύοντα, "serving well".

In LXX verses φῶς occurs together with Hebrew אוֹר 90 times (`word φῶς`). That is same-verse co-occurrence, not a word-by-word alignment. The data doesn't show which Hebrew text the Greek translators had in front of them.

## 4. "Light": the options, side by side

This verse is a contested reading, so here are both options with their basis. Neither is a verdict.

- **(a) No object: "he will see [and] be satisfied."** Basis: the Masoretic text (WLC) יִרְאֶה יִשְׂבָּע. KJV, YLT and RV1909 follow it. 4Q56 has no text at this point.
- **(b) "He will see light and be satisfied."** Basis: 1QIsaa and 1Q8, which read יראה אור. 4Q58 has או at this point (the ר is reconstructed). The Septuagint's δεῖξαι αὐτῷ φῶς, "to show him light", has "light" too, though in a differently built sentence. WEB ("he will see the light") and BSB ("He will see the light of life") follow this reading.

What the immediate context shows: verse 10 uses the same verb with an object, [HEB Isa.53.10] נַפְשׁוֹ יִרְאֶה זֶרַע "…he will see offspring", and verse 11 in WLC uses it with none. Both readings make sense in context. The text allows both.

A second, smaller difference: "my servant" (WLC, 1Q8, 4Q58) or "his servant" (1QIsaa עבדו). The Greek has neither form.

## How published translations handle it

- KJV "He shall see of the travail of his soul, [and] shall be satisfied" and RV1909 "Del trabajo de su alma verá [y] será saciado". Both follow WLC, and both mark the "and" they added in brackets. [added-words, marked by the translators]
- WEB "he will see the light and be satisfied". This follows the DSS/LXX reading without saying so. [added-words relative to WLC: the word comes from other witnesses]
- BSB "He will see the light of life". "Light" comes from the DSS/LXX witnesses. "Of life" is not in any witness the tool returned. Capitals in "His soul", "My righteous Servant". [added-words; capitalization]
- YLT "he seeth — he is satisfied". Follows WLC.

## For your study

- Read the verse aloud in two versions, KJV and WEB, and ask the group: what changes if the servant "sees light"? What stays the same? In every witness shown, he carries the many's wrongs, and many are put in the right.
