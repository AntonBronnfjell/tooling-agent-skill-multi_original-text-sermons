# An Assembly Built, Filled and Entered — Matthew 16:18; Acts 2:4; Acts 2:38

## Introduction

Some Bible words are so familiar that we stop hearing them. "Church." "Holy Ghost." "Be baptized." We may be hearing our own practice instead of the words on the page.

Tonight we take three short texts that every Christian knows: Jesus' promise to Peter, the morning of Pentecost, and Peter's answer to the crowd. We will read each one in Greek, then word for word, then in plain English, and we will ask a simple question each time: what does the word on the page actually say, and where have our translations added something?

## Text

This study uses three passages, one for each point: Matthew 16:18 (Point 1), Acts 2:4 (Point 2) and Acts 2:38 (Point 3). In Matthew, Jesus answers Peter after Peter has said, "You are the Anointed, the son of the living God" (Matt.16.16). In Acts 2, Luke describes the gathering of Jesus' followers at Pentecost and Peter's speech to the crowd.

> [GRC Matt.16.18] κἀγὼ δέ σοι λέγω ὅτι σὺ εἶ Πέτρος, καὶ ἐπὶ ταύτῃ τῇ πέτρᾳ οἰκοδομήσω μου τὴν ἐκκλησίαν,
> Literal: and-I also to-you say that you are Petros, and upon this the rock I-will-build my the assembly,
> Natural: And I tell you: you are Peter ("Rock"), and on this rock I will build my assembly,

> [GRC Matt.16.18] καὶ πύλαι ᾅδου οὐ κατισχύσουσιν αὐτῆς·
> Literal: and gates of-Hades not will-prevail-against it
> Natural: and the gates of Hades will not overpower it.

> [GRC Acts.2.4] καὶ ἐπλήσθησαν πάντες πνεύματος ἁγίου, καὶ ἤρξαντο λαλεῖν ἑτέραις γλώσσαις καθὼς τὸ πνεῦμα ἐδίδου ἀποφθέγγεσθαι αὐτοῖς.
> Literal: and they-were-filled all of-holy spirit, and began to-speak in-other tongues/languages as the spirit was-giving to-utter to-them
> Natural: They were all filled with holy spirit, and began to speak in other languages, as the spirit kept giving them words to speak out.

> [GRC Acts.2.38] Πέτρος δὲ πρὸς αὐτούς· Μετανοήσατε, καὶ βαπτισθήτω ἕκαστος ὑμῶν ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ
> Literal: Peter and to them: repent/turn (you all), and let-be-immersed each of-you upon the name of-Jesus Anointed
> Natural: Peter said to them, "Repent, and let each of you be immersed (or: washed) on the name of Jesus the Anointed,

> [GRC Acts.2.38] εἰς ἄφεσιν τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν ὑμῶν, καὶ λήμψεσθε τὴν δωρεὰν τοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος·
> Literal: into/toward release of-the sins of-you, and you-will-receive the gift of-the holy spirit
> Natural: toward the release of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the holy spirit."

Two edition differences touch these verses. In Acts 2:38 some printed editions (WH, Tregelles) read "in the name" (ἐν) where the others read "on the name" (ἐπὶ); and the TR and Byzantine texts lack "your" after "sins". Neither changes the main sense; details are in the Notes.

## Big Idea

Jesus promises to build an assembly that belongs to him; God's spirit fills its people and gives them speech; and Peter tells his hearers how to join it: turn, and be immersed on Jesus' name.

Three verbs carry it: "I will build" (Matthew), "they were filled" (Acts 2:4), "repent … let each be immersed" (Acts 2:38).

## Point 1

**What Jesus builds: an assembly (Matthew 16:18).**

The word our Bibles render "church" is ἐκκλησίαν. Its plain meaning is "assembly, gathering". How do we know? Not from its parts. You may have heard that ἐκκλησία means "the called-out ones" because of ἐκ ("out") and καλέω ("call"). That is the root fallacy: a word means what its users meant by it, not what its pieces suggest.

Here is the evidence our tool shows. In the Greek Old Testament the word appears 77 times, and it co-occurs in the same verse with the Hebrew קָהָל ("assembly") 66 times; that is same-verse co-occurrence, not a word-for-word alignment. Moses speaks of the law given "on the mountain, on the day of the assembly":

> [LXX Deut.9.10] ἐν τῷ ὄρει ἡμέρᾳ ἐκκλησίας
> Literal: on the mountain on-day of-assembly
> Natural: on the mountain, on the day of the assembly

Luke even uses it of a confused civic crowd in Ephesus: [GRC Acts.19.32] ἦν γὰρ ἡ ἐκκλησία συγκεχυμένη, "for the assembly was in confusion". In the ten contexts our tool shows, the word names a gathering of people; none shows "called out".

So Jesus is not promising a building or an institution. He says μου τὴν ἐκκλησίαν: my assembly, a people gathered around him.

Now the hard part: "this rock". The grammar gives us facts. Πέτρος (Peter's name) is masculine; πέτρᾳ ("rock") is feminine; ταύτῃ is "this", a near demonstrative. From that, readers have seen three options: (a) the rock is Peter himself, by the wordplay Petros/petra; (b) the rock is Peter's statement in 16:16, "you are the Anointed"; (c) the rock is Jesus. The grammar does not settle it, and I will not settle it for you. Notice what is not contested: who builds (Jesus), and whose assembly it is (his).

And the "gates of Hades": the KJV says "hell", but the word is ᾅδου, Hades, the world of the dead. In the Greek Old Testament Jacob mourns that he will go down "to Hades" (Gen.37.35), where the Hebrew has שְׁאֹלָה, "to Sheol". Death's gates will not overpower what Jesus builds.

## Point 2

**What fills the assembly: holy spirit and speech (Acts 2:4).**

The verb ἐπλήσθησαν is passive: "they were filled". They did not fill themselves. And πάντες, "all": no one in the room was left out.

Two notes on our translations. The KJV says "Holy Ghost"; the Greek is πνεύματος ἁγίου, "holy spirit". Manuscripts of the first century had no capital letters, so the capitals in our Bibles are the translator's choice; our Literal and Natural lines leave them out. Also, there is no article here: "filled with holy spirit". The next clause has the article (τὸ πνεῦμα, "the spirit").

What did the spirit do? It gave speech: "began to speak ἑτέραις γλώσσαις". γλῶσσα means "tongue", the body part, and also "language". Which one does Luke mean here? Read on in the chapter: the crowd hears them speak, each in his own dialect (Acts.2.6), and says, "we hear them speaking [GRC Acts.2.11] ταῖς ἡμετέραις γλώσσαις", "in our own languages". The context shows languages the hearers knew. That is why WEB says "other languages".

The verb ἐδίδου is imperfect: "was giving". Luke presents the giving as in progress while they spoke. Here is a skill you can reuse: ask "who is the subject?" The spirit gives; they speak.

## Point 3

**How one enters: turn, and be immersed on his name (Acts 2:38).**

Peter gives two responses. The first is Μετανοήσατε, "repent", second person plural, to the whole crowd. "Repent" is the ordinary English rendering. Its sense comes from usage, not from its parts ("after" + "think"): in the Greek Old Testament, this verb co-occurs in the same verse with Hebrew נָחַם 14 times and שׁוּב ("turn, return") 9 times. Here Peter speaks to people he has just told "you crucified" Jesus, and who ask "what shall we do?" (Acts.2.36-37): the call is to change their mind about him and turn around.

The second is βαπτισθήτω ἕκαστος: third person singular, "let each one be …". Our Bibles say "be baptized", and in our churches "baptize" is a technical term. The Greek verb βαπτίζω is ordinary: in the Greek Old Testament Naaman [LXX 2Kgs.5.14] ἐβαπτίσατο ἐν τῷ Ιορδάνῃ, "dipped himself in the Jordan", and Josephus uses it of a ship that sank (Life.1.15). Our verse does not describe how it was done; so I translate "be immersed (or: washed)" and let the word be as plain as it was.

Then two contested points, and you should see both sides.

First, [GRC Acts.2.38] εἰς ἄφεσιν. The noun ἄφεσις means "release": in the Greek Old Testament it names the jubilee "release" (it co-occurs with יוֹבֵל 18 times; Lev.25.10). The phrase can mean (a) "for, toward release" (purpose) or (b) "with a view to, in connection with release". Second, what does it attach to? "Repent" is plural, "be immersed" is singular, and "your sins" is plural. Some readers take both verbs as leading to release; others tie "release" to the plural "repent". The grammar allows both.

Third, "on the name" (ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματι). Matthew 28:19 has "into the name" of father, son and holy spirit; Acts here has "on the name of Jesus the Anointed". Readers explain the difference as (a) the same practice described with different wording, (b) different formulas in use, or (c) neither book quoting a formula. The texts as printed differ in wording; the data does not show which the writers meant.

And notice the promise that ends the verse: "you will receive the gift of the holy spirit". The same spirit that filled them in 2:4 is offered to the crowd.

## Conclusion

Three texts, one movement: Jesus builds his assembly, the spirit fills it and gives it a voice, and Peter tells people how to enter — turn, and be immersed on Jesus' name.

This week, practice reading under the surface. Take one familiar word — "church", "baptize", "Holy Ghost" — and every time you meet it, say the plain word behind it: assembly, immerse, holy spirit. Notice how the text sounds. And if you belong to Jesus' assembly, remember who builds it: not us. "I will build my assembly, and the gates of Hades will not overpower it."
