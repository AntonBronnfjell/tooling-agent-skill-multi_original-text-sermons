# Three Famous Passages and Their Witnesses — 1 John 5:7–8; Mark 16:9–20; John 7:53–8:11

## Introduction

Open a KJV and a modern Bible side by side, and you will find places where one has words the other lacks, or prints a passage with brackets and a footnote. For some of us that is unsettling: has someone been changing the Bible?

This handout looks at the three best-known cases. For each one we ask: what do the editions built mainly on early manuscripts print, and what do the later printed texts print? The goal is not to shake anyone's trust, but to let you see the evidence for yourself.

## Text

**How to read this handout.** Our data holds printed Greek editions, not individual manuscripts. Four are critical editions built mainly on early manuscripts: NA (Nestle–Aland), SBL, THGNT (Tyndale House) and WH (Westcott–Hort); the variant lists also cite Tregelles (Treg). Two are later texts: Byz (the Byzantine majority text) and TR (the Textus Receptus, the 16th-century printed text behind the KJV and the Spanish RV1909). When I say "early" or "late" below, I mean which editions print a reading, not which manuscripts do; our tools do not show manuscripts.

Three passages, one for each point: 1 John 5:7–8 (Point 1), Mark 16:9–20 (Point 2), John 7:53–8:11 (Point 3).

## Big Idea

When we know which words stand in which editions, we can read each passage honestly: what every edition prints, what only later editions add, and what the editors themselves mark as uncertain.

## Point 1

**1 John 5:7–8: the "three in heaven".**

Here is the text as SBL, NA, THGNT, WH and Byz print it:

> [GRC 1John.5.7] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες,
> Literal: because three are the-ones testifying
> Natural: For there are three who testify:

> [GRC 1John.5.8] τὸ πνεῦμα καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ καὶ τὸ αἷμα, καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
> Literal: the spirit and the water and the blood, and the three into the one are
> Natural: the spirit, the water and the blood, and the three agree as one.

The TR adds words in both verses:

> [GRC:TR 1John.5.7] ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ, ὁ πατήρ, ὁ λόγος, καὶ τὸ ἅγιον πνεῦμα· καὶ οὗτοι οἱ τρεῖς ἕν εἰσι.
> Literal: in the heaven, the father, the word, and the holy spirit; and these the three one are
> Natural: in heaven: the father, the word and the holy spirit; and these three are one.

> [GRC:TR 1John.5.8] Καὶ τρεῖς εἰσὶν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἐν τῇ γῇ,
> Literal: and three are the-ones testifying in the earth
> Natural: And there are three who testify on earth:

What the early editions show: none of them has these words. What the later texts show: only the TR has them. The Byzantine text, the other late text in our data, does not. The variant tool marks every added word as TR-only, class "late". The KJV and RV1909 follow the TR here; BSB and WEB do not; YLT prints the words in brackets.

A skill to take home: when a familiar verse is missing from a modern Bible, check the edition list, not just the label. Here the list is one edition.

## Point 2

**Mark 16:9–20: the longer ending.**

Mark 16:8 ends with the women at the empty tomb:

> [GRC Mark.16.8] καὶ οὐδενὶ οὐδὲν εἶπαν, ἐφοβοῦντο γάρ.
> Literal: and to-no-one nothing they-said, they-were-afraid for
> Natural: And they said nothing to anyone, because they were afraid.

Then verse 9 begins:

> [GRC Mark.16.9] Ἀναστὰς δὲ πρωῒ πρώτῃ σαββάτου ἐφάνη πρῶτον Μαρίᾳ τῇ Μαγδαληνῇ,
> Literal: having-risen and early on-first of-week he-appeared first to-Mary the Magdalene
> Natural: After he rose early on the first day of the week, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene,

What the editions show: all six editions in our data print verses 9–20. But NA, THGNT and WH open the passage with double brackets, [[, before Ἀναστὰς. Double brackets are the editors' way of saying "we judge this a later addition, but we print it". THGNT closes the brackets after the last word of verse 20; SBL, Byz and TR print no brackets at all.

Inside the passage the editions also differ in wording. Some of these change what a reader hears:

- 16:18: NA, THGNT and WH add "and with their hands":

> [GRC:NA Mark.16.18] καὶ ἐν ταῖς χερσὶν ὄφεις ἀροῦσιν,
> Literal: and in the hands snakes they-will-lift
> Natural: and they will pick up snakes with their hands,

  SBL, Byz and TR have only "they will pick up snakes". BSB's "with their hands" follows the first group.
- 16:17: "new" languages (καιναῖς) stands in SBL, NA, Byz and TR; THGNT and WH lack it.
- 16:14: only WH adds "from the dead" after "raised".
- 16:19: SBL, WH, Tregelles, THGNT and NA27 read "the lord Jesus"; NA28, TR and Byz read "the lord".

One more thing the data shows: NA and WH also print, inside verse 8 and in double brackets, a short alternative ending ("they reported briefly to those around Peter…"); SBL prints it without brackets; THGNT, Byz and TR do not have it.

## Point 3

**John 7:53–8:11: the woman caught in adultery.**

The story begins:

> [GRC John.7.53] Καὶ ἐπορεύθη ἕκαστος εἰς τὸν οἶκον αὐτοῦ,
> Literal: and went each to the house of-him
> Natural: Then each of them went home,

and ends, in NA:

> [GRC:NA John.8.11] οὐδὲ ἐγώ σε κατακρίνω· πορεύου καὶ ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν μηκέτι ἁμάρτανε.
> Literal: neither I you condemn; go and from the now no-longer sin
> Natural: "Neither do I condemn you. Go, and from now on do not sin any more."

What the editions show: NA, THGNT and WH open [[ at 7:53; NA and WH close ]] at 8:11. Our data has no THGNT text for 8:11 at all. SBL, Byz and TR print the passage without brackets. Tregelles, cited in the variant lists, is not listed for any word of 7:53–8:11.

Inside the story the wording differs in nearly every verse, and some differences matter:

- 8:9: TR, Byz and SBL add that the accusers left "being convicted by their conscience"; only TR adds "to the last".
- 8:10: TR, Byz and SBL add that Jesus "saw no one but the woman" and asked where "those accusers of yours" were.
- 8:11: NA and WH have "from now on"; Byz and SBL read "I do not judge you" where NA, WH and TR read "I do not condemn you".

## Conclusion

Three passages, three different pictures. The "three in heaven" stand in one late printed text only. Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11 stand in every edition we have, but the critical editors mark them with double brackets as later additions, and the wording inside them varies.

This week: take your own Bible, find these three places, and read the footnotes. Then read the passages aloud as your edition prints them, knowing what is behind the brackets. The evidence is not hidden; it is in front of you. "Go, and from now on do not sin any more."
