## σάρξ in Romans 8:1 — a short word study

**First, a surprise from the text itself.** In the critical editions (SBL, NA, WH, THGNT), Romans 8:1 does not contain σάρξ at all:

> [GRC Rom.8.1] Οὐδὲν ἄρα νῦν κατάκριμα τοῖς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ·
> Literal: no-[thing] therefore now condemnation to-the-ones in Anointed Jesus
> Natural: So now there is no condemnation for those who are in the Anointed, Jesus.

The word appears in Romans 8:1 only in the Textus Receptus (TR) and the Byzantine text (Byz), which add a clause at the end:

> [GRC:TR Rom.8.1] τοῖς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ, μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν, ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα.
> Literal: to-the-ones in Anointed Jesus, not according-to flesh walking, but according-to spirit
> Natural: for those in the Anointed, Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the spirit.

That exact clause is in every edition three verses later, in Romans 8:4:

> [GRC Rom.8.4] ἵνα τὸ δικαίωμα τοῦ νόμου πληρωθῇ ἐν ἡμῖν τοῖς μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα·
> Literal: so-that the requirement of-the law may-be-fulfilled in us, the-ones not according-to flesh walking but according-to spirit
> Natural: so that what the law requires might be fulfilled in us, who walk not according to the flesh but according to the spirit.

So for a class, the honest way to study "σάρξ in Romans 8:1" is: in TR/Byz it is the same phrase as 8:4, and in every edition the phrase belongs to 8:4. Either way, the context that tells us what σάρξ means here is Romans 8:3–9.

**What the word covers (from the tool).** `word σάρξ` glosses it "flesh". It occurs 147 times in the NT (SBL; TR 151, Byz 150) and 158 times in the LXX, where it appears in verses that have Hebrew בָּשָׂר 130 times (same-verse co-occurrence, not alignment). The tool lists 15 different Louw–Nida domain codes for it — a wide word. In the contexts `usage σάρξ` shows, it is used of:

- the body's tissue: LXX Genesis 2:23, "flesh from my flesh"; Josephus, of meat eaten before the blood was drained (Ant.6.120);
- a human couple or a human being as a whole: [LXX Gen.2.24] καὶ ἔσονται οἱ δύο εἰς σάρκα μίαν — "and the two will become one flesh"; Acts 2:17, "on all flesh" — on all people;
- human descent: [GRC Rom.1.3] ἐκ σπέρματος Δαυὶδ κατὰ σάρκα — "from David's seed according to the flesh".

Don't load all of these into Romans 8 (that would be illegitimate totality transfer). Choose the sense the sentence needs.

**What Romans 8 does with it.** In 8:1 (TR/Byz) and 8:4, σάρξ is the object of κατά ("according to") with the verb περιπατοῦσιν, "walk" — here of how people live.

Verse 5 restates the walking with ὄντες, "being". And the phrase is set directly against "according to the spirit" in [GRC Rom.8.4] ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα. Verse 5 explains the contrast in terms of what people set their minds on:

> [GRC Rom.8.5] οἱ γὰρ κατὰ σάρκα ὄντες τὰ τῆς σαρκὸς φρονοῦσιν, οἱ δὲ κατὰ πνεῦμα τὰ τοῦ πνεύματος.
> Literal: the-ones for according-to flesh being the-things of-the flesh think/set-mind-on, the-ones but according-to spirit the-things of-the spirit
> Natural: For those who live by the flesh set their minds on what the flesh wants, but those who live by the spirit on what the spirit wants.

The tool tags σάρξ in 8:4, 8:5, 8:8 and 8:9 with the same domain code (26.7), and gives a different code (58.10) to σάρκα in Romans 1:3 ("descent") and to the last σαρκί of 8:3. That tagging matches the context: in this stretch of Romans 8 σάρξ is not skin or meat, and not ancestry, but a way of living and thinking that the text opposes to living by the spirit. What exactly that "flesh" is beyond this contrast, the passage describes by its results (8:8, those "in flesh" cannot please God) rather than by a definition — so a class should describe it from these verses, not from a dictionary label.

**For the class:** ask "what is σάρξ set against in each verse?" In Genesis 2 nothing — it is simply body; in Romans 1:3 it is set against the next verse's [GRC Rom.1.4] κατὰ πνεῦμα ἁγιωσύνης, "according to a spirit of holiness"; in Romans 8 it is set against πνεῦμα. The contrast word tells you which sense is in play.

### Notes

- Edition difference, Rom.8.1 (`variants Rom.8.1`): TR and Byz add [GRC:TR Rom.8.1] μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν, ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα (Byz the same: [GRC:Byz Rom.8.1] μὴ κατὰ σάρκα περιπατοῦσιν, ἀλλὰ κατὰ πνεῦμα); absent in SBL, NA, WH, THGNT. All seven added words carry the tool label "late" (support TR+Byz only). The data holds printed editions, not individual manuscripts, so I can't tell you which manuscripts have the clause — only which editions print it.
- Meaning: the added words repeat Rom.8.4, which all editions print — so the addition changes 8:1 (it limits "no condemnation" by a description of those who walk), not the chapter's content.
- Comparison (`compare Rom.8.1`): KJV "who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit", WEB and YLT "according to the flesh … according to the Spirit", RV1909 "los que no andan conforme á la carne, mas conforme al espíritu" follow the TR/Byz text [late-reading; also harmonization, because the wording matches Rom.8.4]. BSB ends at "in Christ Jesus" (critical text). KJV/WEB/YLT/BSB "Spirit" with a capital [capitalization]; RV1909 has lower-case "espíritu" in both 8:1 and 8:4. All five render Χριστῷ as "Christ / Cristo" [tradition-vocabulary: "Anointed"].
- Counts: σάρξ NT SBL 147, NA 147, WH 147, THGNT 147, Byz 150, TR 151, all editions 152; LXX 158; Josephus 11, Philo 90 (Josephus/Philo matched by surface form; the tool warns homographs may inflate). - Capitals: "spirit" lower case on the translation lines (no capitals added to πνεῦμα); manuscripts make no upper/lower-case distinction, so capitals are a translator's choice.
