# Every Knee, Every Tongue — Isaiah 45:21–25 behind Philippians 2:10–11

## Introduction

Many of us can finish the sentence "every knee should bow and every tongue confess…" without opening a Bible. Fewer of us know that the words were not new when Paul wrote them. They come from a speech in Isaiah, where God puts himself on oath.

If Paul is borrowing a sentence, the honest way to read him is to read the sentence where he found it first. That is what this study does: we put the Hebrew text of Isaiah 45:21–25 and its old Greek translation, the Septuagint, side by side, verse by verse, and then we lay Philippians 2:10–11 over them and see exactly which words are shared and which are not.

## Text

Paul, writing in the first century to the assembly at Philippi, says:

> [GRC Phil.2.10] ἵνα ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ πᾶν γόνυ κάμψῃ ἐπουρανίων καὶ ἐπιγείων καὶ καταχθονίων,
> Literal: so-that at/in the name of-Jesus every knee may-bend, of-heavenly-ones and earthly-ones and under-the-earth-ones
> Natural: so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in the heavens, on earth and under the earth,

> [GRC Phil.2.11] καὶ πᾶσα γλῶσσα ἐξομολογήσηται ὅτι κύριος Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς εἰς δόξαν θεοῦ πατρός.
> Literal: and every tongue may-acknowledge that lord/Lord [is] Jesus Anointed, to glory of-God father
> Natural: and every tongue should acknowledge that Jesus the Anointed is lord, to the glory of God the father.

All six Greek editions in our data (SBL, NA28, THGNT, WH, Byz, TR) print these two verses with the same words; the variants check returns no differences.

The study takes one block of Isaiah per point: Point 1 is Isaiah 45:21–22, Point 2 is Isaiah 45:23, Point 3 is Isaiah 45:24–25 laid beside Philippians. For each verse you get the Hebrew (the Masoretic text, WLC) and then the Septuagint (Rahlfs).

## Big Idea

In Isaiah the one God, who alone rescues, swears that every knee will bow to him and every tongue will swear (Hebrew) or acknowledge God (Greek); Philippians 2:10–11 uses that same wording of bowing and acknowledging for the name of Jesus, "to the glory of God the father".

Two phrases carry the link: "every knee will bend" and "every tongue will acknowledge". Everything else in this study is about seeing what stands around those phrases in each text.

## Point 1

**Isaiah 45:21–22 — the only rescuer calls the ends of the earth.**

> [HEB Isa.45.21] הַגִּידוּ וְהַגִּישׁוּ אַף יִוָּעֲצוּ יַחְדָּו מִי הִשְׁמִיעַ זֹאת מִקֶּדֶם מֵאָז הִגִּידָהּ הֲלוֹא אֲנִי יְהוָה וְאֵין־עוֹד אֱלֹהִים מִבַּלְעָדַי אֵל־צַדִּיק וּמוֹשִׁיעַ אַיִן זוּלָתִי׃
> Literal: declare and-bring-near, yes let-them-take-counsel together; who made-heard this from-of-old, from-then declared-it? was-it-not I, YHWH? and-there-is-no more god besides-me; a-god righteous and-rescuing, there-is-none except-me
> Natural: Declare it, bring your case; let them consult together. Who announced this long ago, who declared it from the start? Was it not I, YHWH? There is no other god besides me; a righteous god and rescuer, there is none but me.

> [LXX Isa.45.21] εἰ ἀναγγελοῦσιν ἐγγισάτωσαν ἵνα γνῶσιν ἅμα τίς ἀκουστὰ ἐποίησεν ταῦτα ἀπ᾿ ἀρχῆς τότε ἀνηγγέλη ὑμῖν ἐγὼ ὁ θεός καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἄλλος πλὴν ἐμοῦ δίκαιος καὶ σωτὴρ οὐκ ἔστιν πάρεξ ἐμοῦ
> Literal: if they-will-announce, let-them-come-near, so-that they-may-know together who made these-things heard from beginning; then it-was-announced to-you: I the god/God, and not is other besides me; righteous and rescuer not is except me
> Natural: If they will announce it, let them come near, so that together they may know who made these things heard from the beginning. Then it was announced to you: I am God, and there is no other besides me; there is no righteous one and rescuer except me.

> [HEB Isa.45.22] פְּנוּ־אֵלַי וְהִוָּשְׁעוּ כָּל־אַפְסֵי־אָרֶץ כִּי אֲנִי־אֵל וְאֵין עוֹד׃
> Literal: turn to-me and-be-rescued, all ends-of earth; for I [am] God/god and-there-is-no more
> Natural: Turn to me and be rescued, all you ends of the earth, for I am God, and there is no other.

> [LXX Isa.45.22] ἐπιστράφητε πρός με καὶ σωθήσεσθε οἱ ἀπ᾿ ἐσχάτου τῆς γῆς ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ θεός καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἄλλος
> Literal: turn to me and you-will-be-rescued, the-ones from end of-the earth; I am the god/God and not is other
> Natural: Turn to me and you will be rescued, you from the end of the earth. I am God, and there is no other.

Watch the names. In verse 21 the Hebrew has the personal name יְהוָה; the Greek has "I am God", ὁ θεός. The hearers are summoned to declare, come near and consult together, and the claim is that only one announced this long ago and only one rescues. The invitation in verse 22 goes to "all the ends of the earth".

## Point 2

**Isaiah 45:23 — the oath: swear or acknowledge?**

> [HEB Isa.45.23] בִּי נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי יָצָא מִפִּי צְדָקָה דָּבָר וְלֹא יָשׁוּב כִּי־לִי תִּכְרַע כָּל־בֶּרֶךְ תִּשָּׁבַע כָּל־לָשׁוֹן׃
> Literal: by-me I-have-sworn; has-gone-out from-my-mouth rightness, a-word, and-not it-will-return: that to-me will-bow every knee, will-swear every tongue
> Natural: By myself I have sworn; what is right has gone out of my mouth, a word that will not turn back: to me every knee will bow, every tongue will swear.

> [LXX Isa.45.23] κατ᾿ ἐμαυτοῦ ὀμνύω ἦ μὴν ἐξελεύσεται ἐκ τοῦ στόματός μου δικαιοσύνη οἱ λόγοι μου οὐκ ἀποστραφήσονται ὅτι ἐμοὶ κάμψει πᾶν γόνυ καὶ ἐξομολογήσεται πᾶσα γλῶσσα τῷ θεῷ
> Literal: by myself I-swear: truly will-go-out from the mouth of-me righteousness; the words of-me not will-be-turned-back; that to-me will-bend every knee and will-acknowledge every tongue to-the god/God
> Natural: By myself I swear: righteousness will surely go out of my mouth, and my words will not be turned back: to me every knee will bend, and every tongue will acknowledge God.

Here is the clearest difference between the two texts. The Hebrew says every tongue "will swear" (the same verb God uses of himself at the start of the verse). The Greek has every tongue "acknowledge", and adds "to God", τῷ θεῷ, where the Hebrew line has no divine name; the Hebrew's "to me" covers both halves.

What does the Greek verb mean? Our tool shows that ἐξομολογέω occurs 92 times in the LXX, and in LXX verses it co-occurs most with the Hebrew יָדָה (80 times; same verse, not alignment). In the contexts shown it means praising or thanking (Gen 29:35, "I will acknowledge/praise the lord"), acknowledging a name (1 Kgs 8:33), and in Josephus admitting or confessing — wrongs (Ant. 8.129) or one's own folly (War 1.625). The papyrus citations reported for it use it of acknowledging a debt. So "acknowledge, declare openly" is the range; whether praise or admission is in view comes from context.

The Dead Sea Scrolls add little that changes meaning here: the great Isaiah scroll (1QIsaa) reads "and will swear", with an extra "and"; the small fragment 4Q56 keeps only the letters for "knee, will swear".

## Point 3

**Isaiah 45:24–25 and Philippians 2:10–11 — whose name stands where.**

> [HEB Isa.45.24] אַךְ בַּיהוָה לִי אָמַר צְדָקוֹת וָעֹז עָדָיו יָבוֹא וְיֵבֹשׁוּ כֹּל הַנֶּחֱרִים בּוֹ׃
> Literal: only in-YHWH, of-me one-said, [are] righteousnesses and-strength; to-him will-come and-be-ashamed all the-ones-burning against-him
> Natural: "Only in YHWH," it is said of me, "are right deeds and strength." To him they will come, and all who raged against him will be put to shame.

> [LXX Isa.45.24] λέγων δικαιοσύνη καὶ δόξα πρὸς αὐτὸν ἥξουσιν καὶ αἰσχυνθήσονται πάντες οἱ ἀφορίζοντες ἑαυτούς
> Literal: saying: righteousness and glory to him will-come, and will-be-ashamed all the-ones separating themselves
> Natural: saying, "Righteousness and glory will come to him," and all who separate themselves will be put to shame.

> [HEB Isa.45.25] בַּיהוָה יִצְדְּקוּ וְיִתְהַלְלוּ כָּל־זֶרַע יִשְׂרָאֵל׃
> Literal: in/by-YHWH will-be-in-the-right and-will-boast all seed-of Israel
> Natural: In YHWH all the offspring of Israel will be in the right and will boast.

> [LXX Isa.45.25] ἀπὸ κυρίου δικαιωθήσονται καὶ ἐν τῷ θεῷ ἐνδοξασθήσονται πᾶν τὸ σπέρμα τῶν υἱῶν Ισραηλ
> Literal: from lord/Lord they-will-be-made/declared-right and in the god/God will-be-glorified all the seed of-the sons of-Israel
> Natural: From the Lord they will be put in the right, and in God all the offspring of the sons of Israel will be glorified.

Now the divine names verse by verse, exactly as the tools print them. The Hebrew has יְהוָה in verses 21, 24 and 25. The Greek has ὁ θεός in 21, "God" again in 22, τῷ θεῷ in 23 (where the Hebrew has only "to me"), no divine name at all in 24, and κυρίου in 25.

Paul uses the same Isaiah line in Romans, in the Septuagint's wording:

> [GRC Rom.14.11] ὅτι ἐμοὶ κάμψει πᾶν γόνυ, καὶ πᾶσα γλῶσσα ἐξομολογήσεται τῷ θεῷ.
> Literal: that to-me will-bend every knee, and every tongue will-acknowledge to-the god/God
> Natural: to me every knee will bend, and every tongue will acknowledge God.

Lay Philippians over Isaiah's Greek. Shared: πᾶν γόνυ "every knee", a form of κάμπτω "bend", πᾶσα γλῶσσα "every tongue", a form of the verb "acknowledge". Different: Isaiah's "to me … to God" becomes "at the name of Jesus", and what the tongue acknowledges is spelled out: "Jesus the Anointed is lord". Paul adds "in the heavens, on earth and under the earth" and closes "to the glory of God the father".

A grammar habit worth reusing: Isaiah's verbs are futures, a promise ("will bend, will acknowledge"); Paul's are subjunctives after ἵνα, a purpose ("so that every knee should bend"). Same words, different frame.

And κύριος itself? The tool shows its range: a respectful address, "sir" (Acts 1:6 Κύριε); an owner or master (Josephus speaks of someone made "master of Egypt", Ant. 2.174); and, in the Septuagint, the word that stands in verses where the Hebrew has יְהוָה (5,130 same-verse co-occurrences, not alignment; e.g. Gen 2:8). Read in Isaiah's light, κύριος in Philippians 2:11 can be heard (a) as the title "lord, master" given to Jesus; (b) as the word that, in the Septuagint, stands where the Hebrew has the divine name; or (c) both. The shared wording is in the data; which of these Paul meant, the data does not decide.

## Conclusion

Isaiah's oath and Paul's sentence share their most memorable words: every knee bends, every tongue acknowledges. Isaiah says it of the one God who alone rescues, swearing by himself; Paul says it of the name of Jesus, and ends with the glory of God the father. Hold the two side by side and let your class see both.

For this week: read Isaiah 45:18–25 aloud, then Philippians 2:5–11. Underline the words they share. Then ask what each text says the bowing and acknowledging are for.

"To me every knee will bend, and every tongue will acknowledge God."
