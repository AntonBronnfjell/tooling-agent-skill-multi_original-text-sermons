# Three Places Where Our Bibles Differ — 1 John 5:7–8, Mark 16:9–20, John 7:53–8:11

## Introduction

Open a King James Version and a modern translation side by side at 1 John 5:7. One has a sentence about "the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost"; the other does not. Turn to the end of Mark or to the start of John 8 and you will find brackets or a footnote about "the earliest manuscripts". Many of us have seen those notes and turned the page a little uneasy.

This handout slows down at those three places and asks one question: what do the Greek editions actually print — and which wording stands in the editions built on the older evidence, and which appears only in the later printed text? We are not here to defend a translation or to attack one. We are here to learn to read the notes.

## Text

Each point takes one text: Point 1 is 1 John 5:7–8, Point 2 is Mark 16:9–20, Point 3 is John 7:53–8:11. The Greek is quoted inside each point.

First, how we know. Our tool does not hold the ancient manuscripts themselves. It holds six printed Greek editions: four critical editions built mainly on the early manuscripts (NA28, SBL, THGNT, Westcott–Hort = WH), the Byzantine majority text (Byz), and the Textus Receptus (TR), the 16th-century printed text behind the KJV and the Reina-Valera 1909. So in this handout "early" means "printed by the critical editions", and "late" means "printed only by Byz and/or TR". I will not claim anything about individual manuscripts that the tool does not show.

## Big Idea

These are three different cases: in 1 John 5:7–8 a sentence is printed only in the late text; Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11 are printed by every edition, but editors fence them off with double brackets, and inside them the wording itself shifts from edition to edition.

The skill to take home is to tell the cases apart: "only in the TR" is not the same as "bracketed", and "bracketed" is not the same as "every edition has the same words".

## Point 1

**1 John 5:7–8 — a sentence found only in the TR.**

This is what SBL, NA28, THGNT, WH and Byz all print:

> [GRC 1John.5.7] ὅτι τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες,
> Literal: because three are the-ones testifying
> Natural: For there are three that testify:

> [GRC 1John.5.8] τὸ πνεῦμα καὶ τὸ ὕδωρ καὶ τὸ αἷμα, καὶ οἱ τρεῖς εἰς τὸ ἕν εἰσιν.
> Literal: the spirit and the water and the blood, and the three into the one are
> Natural: the spirit, the water and the blood, and the three agree as one.

And this is what the TR adds between "testify" and "the spirit":

> [GRC:TR 1John.5.7] ἕν τῷ οὐρανῷ ὁ πατήρ ὁ λόγος καὶ τὸ ἅγιον πνεῦμα καὶ οὗτοι οἱ τρεῖς ἕν εἰσιν
> Literal: in (printed "one") the heaven the father, the word and the holy spirit, and these the three one are
> Natural: in heaven: the father, the word and the holy spirit; and these three are one.

> [GRC:TR 1John.5.8] καὶ τρεῖς εἰσιν οἱ μαρτυροῦντες ἕν τῇ γῇ,
> Literal: and three are the-ones testifying in (printed "one") the earth
> Natural: And there are three that testify on earth:

How we know: the variants list for these two verses shows every added word, from "in heaven" to "on earth", with a single supporting edition — TR — and the label "late". No other edition in our data prints them. One printing detail: the TR text in our data spells the word for "in" with the accent of the word for "one"; I translated the sense and explain it in the Notes.

So the KJV and the Reina-Valera 1909 follow the TR here; the BSB and WEB follow the other editions; Young's Literal prints the extra words in square brackets. If you teach from this verse, your class should know that the longer sentence rests on the TR alone in the data we have.

## Point 2

**Mark 16:9–20 — printed by every edition, bracketed by editors.**

In the SBL text, verse 8 ends the scene at the tomb this way:

> [GRC Mark.16.8] καὶ οὐδενὶ οὐδὲν εἶπαν, ἐφοβοῦντο γάρ.
> Literal: and to-no-one nothing they-said; they-were-afraid for
> Natural: and they said nothing to anyone, for they were afraid.

Then verse 9 begins:

> [GRC Mark.16.9] Ἀναστὰς δὲ πρωῒ πρώτῃ σαββάτου ἐφάνη πρῶτον Μαρίᾳ τῇ Μαγδαληνῇ,
> Literal: having-risen and early on-first of-week he-appeared first to-Mary the Magdalene
> Natural: Having risen early on the first day of the week, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene,

What the editions show:

- All six editions print verses 9–20. Nothing here is "missing" from the data.
- NA28, THGNT and WH open verse 9 with a double bracket, the editors' sign that they judge the passage a later addition; THGNT closes it after verse 20. Byz and TR show the same brackets in our data, but that marker is inherited in the data, not their own judgment. SBL prints no brackets.
- After "for they were afraid", NA28 and WH also print a second, short ending inside double brackets; SBL prints it without brackets; THGNT, Byz and TR do not print it.
- Inside the passage the wording shifts. Verse 17, "new" tongues: in SBL, NA28, Byz and TR, not in THGNT or WH. Verse 14, "raised from the dead": only WH. Verse 19, "the lord Jesus": SBL, THGNT, WH; NA28, Byz and TR have "the lord". And verse 18:

> [GRC:NA Mark.16.18] καὶ ἐν ταῖς χερσὶν ὄφεις ἀροῦσιν,
> Literal: and in the hands snakes they-will-pick-up
> Natural: and they will pick up snakes with their hands,

"With their hands" stands in NA28, THGNT and WH; SBL, Byz and TR do not have it. That is why the BSB has "with their hands" and the KJV does not.

## Point 3

**John 7:53–8:11 — bracketed, and the wording moves.**

> [GRC John.7.53] Καὶ ἐπορεύθη ἕκαστος εἰς τὸν οἶκον αὐτοῦ,
> Literal: and went each to the house of-him
> Natural: Then each one went to his own house,

NA28, THGNT, WH, Byz and TR print a double bracket before this verse; SBL prints none. At the end, NA28, WH, Byz and TR close the bracket after 8:11; SBL does not; THGNT has no 8:11 at all in our data. Even the first verb shifts: NA28 and WH print "they went", the others "each one went".

Inside the story, the variants list for 8:1–11 has rows in every verse. Three change what you hear. In 8:9, "convicted by their conscience" is printed by TR and Byz (and by the SBL text in our data), not by NA28, THGNT or WH; TR alone adds "to the last". In 8:10, "seeing no one but the woman" and "those accusers of yours" are in TR and Byz (and the SBL text), not in NA28, THGNT or WH. And Jesus' answer in 8:11 comes in two forms:

> [GRC:NA John.8.11] οὐδὲ ἐγώ σε κατακρίνω· πορεύου καὶ ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν μηκέτι ἁμάρτανε.
> Literal: neither I you condemn; go and from the now no-longer sin
> Natural: Neither do I condemn you. Go, and from now on sin no more.

> [GRC John.8.11] Οὐδὲ ἐγώ σε κρίνω πορεύου, καὶ μηκέτι ἁμάρτανε.
> Literal: neither I you judge; go, and no-longer sin
> Natural: Neither do I judge you. Go, and sin no more.

"Condemn" stands in NA28, WH and TR; "judge" in Byz and in the SBL text of our data. "From now on" is only in NA28 and WH. A habit worth reusing: look for the same word nearby. In 8:10 Jesus asks whether anyone "condemned" her, using the same verb as NA28's answer. The two forms of 8:11 say close to the same thing; the editions differ in the word.

## Conclusion

Three passages, three cases. In 1 John 5:7–8 the long sentence is printed only by the TR. In Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11 every edition prints the passage, editors bracket it, and inside it the wording moves.

This week, take your own Bible to these three places. Find the bracket, the footnote or the missing verse number, and decide which case it is: only in the late text, bracketed, or reworded. Then compare one older translation (KJV) with one newer one (BSB or WEB) at Mark 16:18 and John 8:9, and see which edition each is following.

And end where the story ends: "Neither do I condemn you. Go, and from now on sin no more."
