# ἐκκλησία in the first century — a short word study

The word many English Bibles render *church* is ἐκκλησία. Before asking what it means in Paul or Acts, it helps to see how Greek speakers and writers were using it before and around the New Testament. Everything below comes from the evidence our tools return, not from a dictionary summary.

**The numbers.** Our data counts ἐκκλησία 117 times in the New Testament, 77 in the Septuagint (the Greek Old Testament), 48 in Josephus and 23 in Philo (Josephus/Philo matches are by surface form, so the tool warns they may be slightly off). In Septuagint verses it co-occurs with the Hebrew קָהָל ("assembly, gathering") 66 times — that is same-verse co-occurrence, not a word-for-word alignment.

## 1. In the Septuagint: Israel gathered — and other gatherings

In Deuteronomy the speaker recalls the day at Horeb when he was told to gather the people:

> [LXX Deut.4.10] τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῆς ἐκκλησίας ὅτε εἶπεν κύριος πρός με ἐκκλησίασον πρός με τὸν λαόν
> Literal: on-the day of-the assembly when said lord/the Lord to me: assemble to me the people
> Natural: on the day of the assembly, when the Lord said to me, "Gather the people to me"

The Hebrew of the same verse uses the verb from the same family as קָהָל:

> [HEB Deut.4.10] הַקְהֶל־לִי אֶת־הָעָם
> Literal: assemble to-me the people
> Natural: gather the people to me

Here the ἐκκλησία is an event: the day the people stood gathered to hear. But the word is not reserved for holy gatherings. A psalm uses it for the opposite:

> [LXX Ps.26.5] ἐμίσησα ἐκκλησίαν πονηρευομένων
> Literal: I-hated assembly of-evildoers
> Natural: I hate the gathering of evildoers

## 2. In Josephus: assemblies of the people, in Israel's story and in his own century

Josephus, a Jewish historian writing in Greek in the first century, uses it for Moses gathering the people (the speaker is named as Moses in Ant. 3.83):

> [EXT:Josephus Ant.3.84] συγκαλεῖ τὸ πλῆθος εἰς ἐκκλησίαν ἀκουσόμενον ὧν ὁ θεὸς εἴποι πρὸς αὐτόν
> Literal: he-calls-together the multitude into assembly to-hear what the God said to him
> Natural: he calls the people together into an assembly to hear what God had said to him

He uses the same word for public meetings in Jerusalem — a king gathering the whole population, and a crowd meeting during the war years (background: *Jewish War* book 4 covers that period):

> [EXT:Josephus Ant.16.62] συνήγαγεν ἐκκλησίαν πάνδημον·
> Literal: he-gathered assembly of-all-the-people
> Natural: he gathered an assembly of the whole people.

> [EXT:Josephus War.4.162] Καὶ δὴ συνελθόντος τοῦ πλήθους εἰς ἐκκλησίαν
> Literal: and indeed having-come-together the multitude into assembly
> Natural: And when the crowd had come together into an assembly,

The Moulton–Milligan entry in our data (noisy OCR of a printed lexicon, so paraphrased, not quoted) reports two inscriptions: a bilingual inscription of AD 103–4 from the theatre at Ephesus about statues to be set up for the assembly in the theatre, where the Latin side simply borrows the word as *ecclesia*; and an inscription from Apamea mentioning an assembly of the whole people.

## 3. In the New Testament: the same word, the same range

Luke uses it of the city crowd that rushed into the theatre at Ephesus (Acts 19:29, 35) — twice in one scene, once for a confused mob and once for the lawful assembly:

> [GRC Acts.19.32] ἦν γὰρ ἡ ἐκκλησία συγκεχυμένη,
> Literal: was for the assembly confused
> Natural: for the assembly was in confusion,

> [GRC Acts.19.39] ἐν τῇ ἐννόμῳ ἐκκλησίᾳ ἐπιλυθήσεται.
> Literal: in the lawful assembly it-will-be-settled
> Natural: it will be settled in the lawful assembly.

Stephen uses it for Israel at Sinai, as the Septuagint did:

> [GRC Acts.7.38] οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ γενόμενος ἐν τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ
> Literal: this is the-one having-been in the assembly in the desert
> Natural: This is the one who was in the assembly in the desert

And for the gathered followers of Jesus in Jerusalem — people who met in houses:

> [GRC Acts.8.3] Σαῦλος δὲ ἐλυμαίνετο τὴν ἐκκλησίαν κατὰ τοὺς οἴκους εἰσπορευόμενος,
> Literal: Saul and was-ravaging the assembly, house-by-house entering
> Natural: But Saul was ravaging the assembly, going into house after house,

## What the evidence shows — and what it doesn't

- In the contexts shown here (LXX Deut 4:10, Ps 26:5; Josephus Ant. 3.84, 16.62, War 4.162; Acts 7:38, 8:3, 19:32, 19:39; the two inscriptions), ἐκκλησία names a gathering of people, or the people who gather: Israel before God, a gathering of evildoers, a city's or a nation's public assembly, a confused crowd, a lawful civic meeting, the followers of Jesus.
- None of these contexts is a building, and none shows the sense "called-out ones". You may have heard ἐκκλησία explained from its parts (ἐκ "out" + καλέω "call"); that is the root fallacy — a word means what its users meant by it, and the contexts we have point to "assembly, gathering". Nothing here tells us how every first-century hearer understood the word.
- What made a particular ἐκκλησία distinctive in each passage came from the words around it (whose assembly, gathered for what), not from the noun itself.

**For your class:** when you meet "church" in your Bible, try reading "assembly" or "gathering" and ask the passage: *whose* assembly, gathered *where*, and *for what*? Compare Acts 7:38, where the KJV has "the church in the wilderness" while BSB, WEB and YLT have "the assembly in the wilderness" for the same Greek word it renders "assembly" in Acts 19:32.
