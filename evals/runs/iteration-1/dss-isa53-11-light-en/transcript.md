# Transcript — dss-isa53-11-light-en

**User:** For a Bible study in English with a Christian audience (specialization: teaching), explain what Isaiah 53:11 says in the Hebrew, in the Septuagint and in the Dead Sea Scrolls. I heard some manuscripts say 'light'. Show me the readings.

---

**Assistant:**

You heard right. "Light" is in some of the oldest witnesses to this verse. The Masoretic Hebrew text doesn't have it. Since this is a single-verse question I've answered here in chat and haven't written a sermon file. Every Hebrew and Greek form below was copied from the skill's database in this session.

### 1. The Hebrew (Masoretic text, Leningrad codex, c. 1008)

> [HEB Isa.53.11] מֵעֲמַל נַפְשׁוֹ יִרְאֶה יִשְׂבָּע בְּדַעְתּוֹ יַצְדִּיק צַדִּיק עַבְדִּי לָרַבִּים וַעֲוֹנֹתָם הוּא יִסְבֹּל׃
> Literal: from-the-toil-of his-soul/self he-will-see, he-will-be-satisfied; by-his-knowledge will-make/declare-right [the] righteous-one my-servant for-the-many, and-their-iniquities he will-bear
> Natural: After the toil of his life he will see and be satisfied. By his knowledge my servant, the righteous one, will put the many in the right, and he will carry their wrongdoings.

The point to notice is יִרְאֶה, "he will see". In this text it has **no object**: he will see — see what? The next verb follows without "and": יִשְׂבָּע, "he will be satisfied". Both are yiqtol forms (`Vqi3ms`), "will see / will be satisfied", and they're not a strict future tense (yiqtol marks an incomplete action, and context decides the time). One verse earlier the same verb does take an object: יִרְאֶה זֶרַע, "he will see offspring" (Isa.53.10).

### 2. The Dead Sea Scrolls (c. 3rd c. BC – 1st c. AD)

The database has this verse in four scrolls. Scroll text has consonants only, without the later vowel signs, and I quote only the letters that are actually on the leather.

> [DSS:1QIsaa Isa.53.11] מעמל נפשוה יראה אור וישבע ובדעתו יצדיק צדיק עבדו לרבים ועוונותם הואה יסבול
> Literal: from-toil-of his-soul he-will-see light and-he-will-be-satisfied, and-by-his-knowledge will-make-right [the] righteous-one his-servant [for] the-many, and-their-iniquities he will-bear
> Natural: After the toil of his life he will see light and be satisfied; and by his knowledge the righteous one, his servant, will put the many in the right, and he will carry their wrongdoings.

The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaa) has אור, "light", as the object of "he will see". It also differs from the Masoretic text in two smaller ways. It joins the verbs with "and" (וישבע ובדעתו), and it reads עבדו, "**his** servant", where the Masoretic text has עַבְדִּי, "**my** servant". Its other differences are only spelling: it writes extra vowel letters, as in נפשוה, הואה and יסבול.

> [DSS:1Q8 Isa.53.11] מעמל נפשו יראה אור
> Literal: from-toil-of his-soul he-will-see light
> Natural: After the toil of his life he will see light

A second Isaiah scroll from Cave 1 (1Q8) also reads אור. After this the fragment is broken. The tool's comparison shows several words missing there, but that is likely lost leather, not an omission.

> [DSS:4Q58 Isa.53.11] מעמל נפשו יראה או ושבע בדעתו
> Literal: from-toil-of his-soul he-will-see li[…] and-he-will-be-satisfied by-his-knowledge
> Natural: After the toil of his life he will see … and be satisfied by his knowledge

In 4Q58 only the letters או survive after "he will see". They match the start of אור, but the rest of the word is gone, so this scroll is only partial support. Like 1QIsaa, it joins the next verb with "and" (ושבע).

The fourth scroll, 4Q56, keeps only a few letters of this verse (א … יסב), not enough to tell either way.

### 3. The Septuagint (Greek translation, 3rd–1st c. BC)

> [LXX Isa.53.11] ἀπὸ τοῦ πόνου τῆς ψυχῆς αὐτοῦ δεῖξαι αὐτῷ φῶς καὶ πλάσαι τῇ συνέσει δικαιῶσαι δίκαιον εὖ δουλεύοντα πολλοῖς καὶ τὰς ἁμαρτίας αὐτῶν αὐτὸς ἀνοίσει
> Literal: from the toil of-the soul of-him, to-show to-him light and to-form [him] with-the understanding, to-make-right a-righteous-one well serving many; and the sins of-them he will-carry
> Natural: [The Lord wishes to take away] the toil of his soul, to show him light and to shape him with understanding, to put in the right a righteous one who serves many well; and he himself will carry their sins.

The Greek has φῶς, "light", too. Its grammar is built differently, though. The infinitives ("to show… to form… to make right") hang on the end of verse 10:

> [LXX Isa.53.10] καὶ βούλεται κύριος ἀφελεῖν
> Literal: and wishes [the] Lord to-take-away
> Natural: and the Lord wishes to take away

So in the Greek the Lord shows the servant light. In the Hebrew and the scrolls, the servant sees light. The Greek also differs elsewhere in the verse: "to form with understanding" where the Masoretic text has "be satisfied by his knowledge", and "serving many well" where it has "my servant … for the many".

### 4. The readings side by side

| witness | after "he will see" | date of the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Masoretic text (WLC) | — (no object) | codex c. 1008 |
| 1QIsaa | אור "light" | scroll, c. 3rd c. BC – 1st c. AD range |
| 1Q8 | אור "light" | same range |
| 4Q58 | או… (partial) | same range |
| LXX (Rahlfs) | φῶς "light" ("to show him light") | translation, 3rd–1st c. BC |

Options (this is a contested reading):

- **(a) "he will see and be satisfied"**: the Masoretic text. The verb can stand without an object, or you can supply one from context, such as the "offspring" he sees in v. 10.
- **(b) "he will see light and be satisfied"**: 1QIsaa, 1Q8, probably 4Q58, and the LXX, which comes at it by its own route ("to show him light").

What the evidence shows: "light" is found in two independent kinds of witness, Hebrew scrolls and the Greek translation. Both are more than a thousand years older than the Leningrad codex. The Masoretic reading is still a real, readable text. The data doesn't tell us how the difference arose, and I won't guess. For your group, the honest summary is that both readings exist, the older witnesses lean towards "light", and the verse's core claims are the same in all of them: after his toil he will see and be satisfied, put many in the right, and carry their wrongdoings.

### 5. What your Bibles do with it

- KJV, RV1909 and YLT follow the Masoretic text, with no "light". KJV prints "[and] shall be satisfied" in brackets, which is the translators' own admission that the word was added.
- WEB reads "he will see the light", following the scrolls and the LXX. It's a choice of text, not a paraphrase.
- BSB reads "He will see the light **of life**". "Of life" is in none of the witnesses above [added-words]. BSB also capitalizes "His soul", "My righteous Servant" [capitalization].
