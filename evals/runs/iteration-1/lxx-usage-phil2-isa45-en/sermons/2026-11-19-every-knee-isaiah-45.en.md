# Every Knee, Every Tongue — Philippians 2:10–11 and Isaiah 45:21–25

## Introduction

When Paul writes "every knee should bow … every tongue confess", many of us hear a phrase we already know. Paul's first readers heard an older text inside it. Tonight we will lay that older text out in two forms — the Hebrew text and the Greek translation (the Septuagint) that Paul's assemblies read — and then ask a reading question we can reuse anywhere: when a writer echoes another text, which words are actually shared, and what changes?

## Text

Isaiah 45 is a court scene. YHWH challenges the nations and their idols: who told these things long ago? (On the divine name: the Hebrew has the four letters יְהוָה; I write YHWH. Many Bibles print "the LORD".)

> [HEB Isa.45.21] הֲלוֹא אֲנִי יְהוָה וְאֵין־עוֹד אֱלֹהִים מִבַּלְעָדַי אֵל־צַדִּיק וּמוֹשִׁיעַ אַיִן זוּלָתִי׃
> Literal: [is it] not I YHWH? and-there-is-no more god apart-from-me; a-God righteous and-saving there-is-none besides-me
> Natural: Was it not I, YHWH? There is no other god apart from me — a righteous God and a rescuer; there is none besides me.

> [LXX Isa.45.21] ἐγὼ ὁ θεός καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἄλλος πλὴν ἐμοῦ δίκαιος καὶ σωτὴρ οὐκ ἔστιν πάρεξ ἐμοῦ
> Literal: I [am] the God and not is other except me; righteous and rescuer not is besides me
> Natural: I am God, and there is no other except me; righteous and a rescuer — there is none besides me.

> [HEB Isa.45.22] פְּנוּ־אֵלַי וְהִוָּשְׁעוּ כָּל־אַפְסֵי־אָרֶץ כִּי אֲנִי־אֵל וְאֵין עוֹד׃
> Literal: turn to-me and-be-saved/rescued all ends-of earth, for I [am] God and-there-is-no more
> Natural: Turn to me and be rescued, all the ends of the earth, for I am God and there is no other.

> [LXX Isa.45.22] ἐπιστράφητε πρός με καὶ σωθήσεσθε οἱ ἀπ᾿ ἐσχάτου τῆς γῆς ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ θεός καὶ οὐκ ἔστιν ἄλλος
> Literal: turn to me and you-will-be-saved/rescued, those from [the] end of-the earth; I am the God and not is other
> Natural: Turn to me and you will be rescued, you from the end of the earth; I am God, and there is no other.

> [HEB Isa.45.23] בִּי נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי יָצָא מִפִּי צְדָקָה דָּבָר וְלֹא יָשׁוּב כִּי־לִי תִּכְרַע כָּל־בֶּרֶךְ תִּשָּׁבַע כָּל־לָשׁוֹן׃
> Literal: by-me I-swore; went-out from-my-mouth righteousness/in-righteousness a-word and-not it-will-return: that to-me will-bow every knee, will-swear every tongue
> Natural: By myself I have sworn; from my mouth has gone out in righteousness a word that will not turn back: to me every knee will bow, every tongue will swear.

> [LXX Isa.45.23] κατ᾿ ἐμαυτοῦ ὀμνύω ἦ μὴν ἐξελεύσεται ἐκ τοῦ στόματός μου δικαιοσύνη οἱ λόγοι μου οὐκ ἀποστραφήσονται ὅτι ἐμοὶ κάμψει πᾶν γόνυ καὶ ἐξομολογήσεται πᾶσα γλῶσσα τῷ θεῷ
> Literal: by myself I-swear: truly will-go-out from the mouth of-me righteousness; the words of-me not will-be-turned-back, that to-me will-bend every knee and will-acknowledge every tongue to-the God
> Natural: By myself I swear: righteousness will surely go out from my mouth, my words will not be turned back — that to me every knee will bend and every tongue will acknowledge God.

> [HEB Isa.45.24] אַךְ בַּיהוָה לִי אָמַר צְדָקוֹת וָעֹז עָדָיו יָבוֹא וְיֵבֹשׁוּ כֹּל הַנֶּחֱרִים בּוֹ׃
> Literal: only in-YHWH, of-me/to-me he-said, righteousnesses and-strength; to-him he-will-come and-will-be-ashamed all those-burning against-him
> Natural: "Only in YHWH," one will say of me, "are righteous acts and strength." To him people will come, and all who burned with anger against him will be ashamed.

> [LXX Isa.45.24] λέγων δικαιοσύνη καὶ δόξα πρὸς αὐτὸν ἥξουσιν καὶ αἰσχυνθήσονται πάντες οἱ ἀφορίζοντες ἑαυτούς
> Literal: saying: righteousness and glory to him will-come, and will-be-ashamed all the [ones] separating themselves
> Natural: saying, "Righteousness and glory will come to him," and all who set themselves apart will be ashamed.

> [HEB Isa.45.25] בַּיהוָה יִצְדְּקוּ וְיִתְהַלְלוּ כָּל־זֶרַע יִשְׂרָאֵל׃
> Literal: in/by-YHWH they-will-be-in-the-right and-they-will-boast all seed-of Israel
> Natural: In YHWH all the offspring of Israel will be in the right and will boast.

> [LXX Isa.45.25] ἀπὸ κυρίου δικαιωθήσονται καὶ ἐν τῷ θεῷ ἐνδοξασθήσονται πᾶν τὸ σπέρμα τῶν υἱῶν Ισραηλ
> Literal: from [the] lord they-will-be-made/declared-right and in the God they-will-be-glorified all the seed of-the sons of-Israel
> Natural: From the Lord all the offspring of the sons of Israel will be put in the right, and in God they will be glorified.

Now Paul, writing to Philippi:

> [GRC Phil.2.10] ἵνα ἐν τῷ ὀνόματι Ἰησοῦ πᾶν γόνυ κάμψῃ ἐπουρανίων καὶ ἐπιγείων καὶ καταχθονίων,
> Literal: so-that at the name of-Jesus every knee may-bend, of-heavenly-ones and earthly-ones and under-the-earth-ones
> Natural: so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend — in the heavens, on earth and under the earth —

> [GRC Phil.2.11] καὶ πᾶσα γλῶσσα ἐξομολογήσηται ὅτι κύριος Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς εἰς δόξαν θεοῦ πατρός.
> Literal: and every tongue may-acknowledge that lord [is] Jesus Anointed, to glory of-God father
> Natural: and every tongue acknowledge that Jesus the Anointed is lord, to the glory of God the father.

## Big Idea

Paul takes the oath YHWH swears about himself in Isaiah 45 — every knee bending, every tongue acknowledging — and sets it around the name of Jesus, "to the glory of God the father".

Two pairs of words carry this: "every knee … bend" and "every tongue … acknowledge". Both are word-for-word in the Greek of Isaiah 45:23 and in Philippians 2:10–11.

## Point 1

**See what is shared — and in which text.**

Put the three lines side by side. The Hebrew says every tongue "will swear" (תִּשָּׁבַע). The Greek of Isaiah says every tongue "will acknowledge" (ἐξομολογήσεται) and adds "to God". Paul's wording follows the Greek: κάμψῃ … ἐξομολογήσηται. So Paul is echoing Isaiah in the form his readers held in their hands.

*How we know:* `lookup.py lxx Isa.45.23` prints the Greek next to the Hebrew. And `lookup.py word ἐξομολογέω` shows that in the Septuagint this verb appears 92 times, most often in verses where the Hebrew has יָדָה (80 verses) — a verb of praising, thanking, acknowledging. That is same-verse co-occurrence, not word-by-word alignment, but it tells us the Greek verb lives in the world of public praise, not only private admission.

## Point 2

**See what changes.**

In Isaiah, the knee bends "to me" — the speaker who has just said "I am God, and there is no other". Paul quotes the same oath in Romans 14:11 and keeps it pointed at God. In Philippians the knees bend "at the name of Jesus", and the tongues acknowledge "that Jesus the Anointed is lord".

Notice also the word κύριος, "lord". In the Greek of Isaiah 45:25 it stands where the Hebrew has the divine name. Readers have drawn different conclusions from this transfer of wording about how Paul relates Jesus to YHWH; the passage itself shows the transfer and then adds a goal: "to the glory of God the father". Both halves are in the text; teach both.

## Point 3

**See how the witnesses differ — and don't hide it.**

The Hebrew has the divine name three times in these verses (vv. 21, 24, 25). The Greek has "God" in v. 21, no divine name at all in v. 24, and "lord" (plus "in God") in v. 25. In v. 24 the Greek also reads "glory" where the Hebrew has "strength", and its verb "will come" is plural; the Hebrew verb is singular. The Isaiah scroll from Qumran (1QIsaa) also has a plural there. None of these changes the oath in v. 23, but they show that Paul's Greek Bible was a translation with its own wording.

## Conclusion

Paul's hymn is built from Isaiah's oath. The shared words are clear: every knee, every tongue. What changes is clear too: the name, and the confession that Jesus is lord, ending in the father's glory.

Practice this week: read Isaiah 45:18–25 aloud, then Philippians 2:5–11. Mark every phrase they share. Ask, each time: who is speaking, and to whom do the knees bend? "Every tongue acknowledge that Jesus the Anointed is lord, to the glory of God the father."
