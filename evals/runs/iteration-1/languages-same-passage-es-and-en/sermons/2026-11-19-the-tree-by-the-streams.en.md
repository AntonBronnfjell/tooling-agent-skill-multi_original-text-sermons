# The Tree by the Streams — Psalm 1:1–3

## Introduction

Everyone is trying to be happy. Advertisers promise it, apps measure it, and most of us chase it by adding things: more money, more approval, more options. Psalm 1 is the front door of the whole book of Psalms, and it opens with that very word, "happy". But it describes a happiness that grows more by what you stop doing and by what you keep chewing on than by what you add.

Tonight we read the first three verses slowly, from the Hebrew, and I want to give you a few reading habits you can take to any psalm.

## Text

> [HEB Ps.1.1] אַשְׁרֵי־הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר׀ לֹא הָלַךְ בַּעֲצַת רְשָׁעִים
> Literal: happinesses-of the-man/person who not walked in-counsel-of wicked-[ones]
> Natural: How happy is the person who does not walk by the advice of the wicked,

> [HEB Ps.1.1] וּבְדֶרֶךְ חַטָּאִים לֹא עָמָד וּבְמוֹשַׁב לֵצִים לֹא יָשָׁב
> Literal: and-in-way-of sinners not stood, and-in-seat-of mockers not sat
> Natural: who does not stand on the road sinners take, or sit down where mockers sit.

> [HEB Ps.1.2] כִּי אִם בְּתוֹרַת יְהוָה חֶפְצוֹ וּבְתוֹרָתוֹ יֶהְגֶּה יוֹמָם וָלָיְלָה
> Literal: but rather in-instruction-of YHWH delight-his, and-in-instruction-his he-murmurs by-day and-night
> Natural: Instead, YHWH's instruction is what he delights in, and he murmurs over that instruction day and night.

> [HEB Ps.1.3] וְהָיָה כְּעֵץ שָׁתוּל עַל־פַּלְגֵי מָיִם
> Literal: and-he-is/will-be like-tree planted by-channels-of water
> Natural: He is like a tree planted beside channels of water,

> [HEB Ps.1.3] אֲשֶׁר פִּרְיוֹ׀ יִתֵּן בְּעִתּוֹ וְעָלֵהוּ לֹא־יִבּוֹל וְכֹל אֲשֶׁר־יַעֲשֶׂה יַצְלִיחַ
> Literal: which fruit-its gives in-time-its, and-leaf-its not withers; and-all that he-does it-succeeds/he-makes-succeed
> Natural: that gives its fruit in its season and whose leaf does not wither. Everything he does succeeds.

The divine name is the four consonants YHWH; most English Bibles print "the LORD".

## Big Idea

The happy person is shaped by what he refuses and rooted by what he keeps repeating: YHWH's instruction, murmured day and night, makes him like a tree by the water.

The words that carry it: "happy", "not … not … not", "murmurs", and "planted".

## Point 1

**Happy by refusing (v. 1).**

The psalm begins with אַשְׁרֵי. The form is plural, and Young's Literal Translation tries to show that: "O the happiness of that one". Our tool's lexicon glosses it "blessed". Either "blessed" or "happy" is a fair rendering; I use "happy" because the word describes the state of the person, not a ritual.

Now a reading habit: watch the verbs. "Walked … stood … sat." In Hebrew these are the form called the perfect; it is not simply past tense. It presents the action as a whole, and in a poem like this it describes what such a person characteristically does not do. And notice the movement: walking by someone's advice, then standing on their road, then sitting down in their seat. Each step settles in a little more. The progression is in the text; the warning is in the progression.

The last group is "mockers", people who sneer. The Greek translation of the psalm calls them λοιμῶν, a word our tool glosses "pestilence": people who are a plague.

## Point 2

**Rooted by repeating (v. 2).**

"But rather": the Hebrew turns hard. The happy person delights in YHWH's תּוֹרָה. We often read "law". The tool's lexicon gloss is "instruction", and that wider sense fits a poem that is about how to live.

Then the verb יֶהְגֶּה, usually "meditates". Our tool glosses this verb "to mutter". How did ancient writers use it? Isaiah uses the same form for a lion growling over its prey:

> [HEB Isa.31.4] כַּאֲשֶׁר יֶהְגֶּה הָאַרְיֵה
> Literal: just-as growls the-lion
> Natural: as a lion growls

In Isaiah 38:14 it is the moaning of a dove. So in Psalm 1 this is not silent emptying of the mind; it is a low, audible murmuring. The person goes over the words under his breath, "by day and by night", with the same kind of low sound a lion makes over its prey. That is a reading habit worth stealing: read out loud, and read it again.

## Point 3

**Planted, not self-made (v. 3).**

"Like a tree planted." The word is a passive participle: somebody planted it. It did not grow there by accident; it was put by "channels of water". Our tool glosses those "streams"; the picture is water that keeps coming in a dry land.

The tree gives fruit "in its season", not every week. Its leaf does not wither.

Then the last line, and here the grammar leaves a choice. The verb יַצְלִיחַ is a causative form, third person. It can mean "everything he does succeeds", or "everything he does, he makes succeed". Young's Literal Translation takes the second; KJV, BSB and WEB take the first. Either way, the success belongs to the planted tree, and the planting came first.

## Conclusion

Happiness in Psalm 1 does not come by adding more. It comes by refusing the advice that slowly seats you among mockers, and by keeping YHWH's instruction on your lips until it soaks into your roots.

Here is your practice for this week. Choose one short passage, Psalm 1:1–3 will do, and read it aloud every morning and every night. Murmur it while you drive or walk. By Sunday, notice what has started to grow.

He is like a tree planted beside channels of water, that gives its fruit in its season.
